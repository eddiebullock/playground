## Question

When a model is given a real person's demographic profile, does it reproduce how real autistic and non-autistic adults answer the AQ, EQ, SQ-R and SPQ: item distributions, inter-item structure, within-group spread, and known group differences?

Primary ground truth is the Channel 4 short forms. YourBrainType and CARD are out-of-distribution replications. Study A1 does not use the Study A2 test split.

## Personas

Coded labels only. No user id, free text, or handwriting of a rare combination. Labels are the participant-facing strings in the appendix.

1. **No persona.** "Answer as yourself."
2. **Demographics only.** Age band, sex, education, occupation, region. Diagnosis is not mentioned. Profiles are still drawn within diagnosis group, so condition 2 and condition 3 differ in what is written, not in who was sampled.
3. **Demographics plus diagnoses.** Condition 2, plus every diagnosis code that is present, in code order. Autism subtype is added when a subtype code is present. This is the primary condition for H1–H5.
4. **Diagnosis only.** One of these sentences, with no demographics: "You are an autistic adult." / "You are an adult with no psychiatric diagnosis."

Age bands match the split: 18–29, 30–44, 45–59, 60–90.

User message, condition 3 (conditions 1, 2, and 4 delete the lines that do not apply):

```
Answer as the person described below. For each item, write the item number and exactly one of these phrases: strongly agree, slightly agree, slightly disagree, strongly disagree. Do not add anything else.

Person:
Age: 18-29
Sex: Female
Education: Undergraduate degree
Occupation: Healthcare
Region: London (England)
Diagnoses: Autism Spectrum Disorder; Depression
Autism detail: Asperger Syndrome (AS)

Items:
1. <item text>
...
40. <item text>
```

Condition 1 replaces the person block with "Answer as yourself." Condition 4 replaces it with the single diagnosis sentence. Item order is the Channel 4 file order: `spq_1`–`spq_10`, `eq_1`–`eq_10`, `sqr_1`–`sqr_10`, `aq_1`–`aq_10`. Wording is the four tuples in `src/scog/scoring/short_forms.py`. The response phrases are shown in that order, which is the order named in the variable-key letter. Scoring treats that order as codes 1–4. That coding is still inferred. It is the direction that reproduces the audit AQ-10 figures; the reversed map does not.

Generation: temperature 1, top_p 1, max tokens 800, the generation seed above. Base and pretrained checkpoints get this text as a raw completion, with no chat template. Instruction-tuned checkpoints get the vendor chat template and the same user text. For Qwen3, thinking is off (`enable_thinking=False`, as documented for that chat template in the Qwen3 technical report). Closed models use the vendor's documented switch for minimum or no extra reasoning; the exact parameter is written into `A1_deviations.md` before freeze.

A line parses when, after removing the item number and folding case and whitespace, it equals one of the four phrases. Anything else is unparseable. An API safety stop is a refusal. Both are counted. A persona is complete only when all 40 items parse. Marginals, scale scores, correlations, and group effects all use complete personas. Incomplete personas stay in the refusal table. A model whose complete rate is below 50% is dropped from the confirmatory means and from any pair that includes it. The drop is reported.

## Hypotheses

Each hypothesis has one primary p-value. Holm (1979) adjusts those five p-values, familywise alpha 0.05. Tests are one-sided in the predicted direction. The primary condition is condition 3. The primary dataset is the Channel 4 confirmatory split. Per-model tables are descriptive. The confirmatory mean is the unweighted mean across models that pass the 50% complete-rate rule.

A human noise ceiling is the same metric on human samples of size N from the eligible confirmatory pool, 1,000 draws, 2.5th to 97.5th percentiles. Distance metrics use two disjoint human samples when the pool has at least 2N rows, and bootstrap samples otherwise. A simulated result is at human level on a metric only when it sits inside that interval. The reported 95% CI is a 1,000-draw bootstrap of the simulated personas.

Bootstrap p-values use the +1 correction: `(1 + #{draws at least as extreme as observed}) / 1001`.

H4 is a prediction of no reliable scale gain, so its p-value is the p-value of the claim that larger models are more faithful. H4 is supported when that Holm-adjusted test does not reject. Holm makes a scale gain harder to declare, which makes H4 easier to retain. That asymmetry is accepted here and reported with the result.

### H1, flattened structure

Simulated respondents match item means better than inter-item correlations.

- Marginal distance: mean, over the 40 items and then over the two diagnosis groups, of Jensen–Shannon divergence in bits (log base 2) between the simulated and human 4-category distributions. The implementation is `scog.metrics.divergence.js_divergence`.
- Structure distance: Frobenius norm of the difference of the off-diagonal polychoric correlations (40 × 40).
- Ceiling-relative z: `(observed − mean of the human-human draws) / sd of those draws`.
- Statistic: z_structure − z_marginal, averaged across models.
- Supported when the Holm-adjusted p for this difference is < 0.05 and the bootstrap 95% CI lies entirely above 0.

Wasserstein distance on codes {1, 2, 3, 4}, Tucker's congruence, and CFA fit are reported and are not in the H1 p-value. Polychoric correlations are two-step maximum likelihood. Factor count k is chosen once, on the exploratory split only, by Horn parallel analysis (95th percentile of eigenvalues from 100 random column-permuted datasets) and then frozen in `A1_deviations.md`. Confirmatory EFAs use that k. Simulated loadings are orthogonal-Procrustes rotated onto the confirmatory human loadings before Tucker's congruence. Congruence is the mean across factors. The CFA pattern is that exploratory solution fit to the simulated confirmatory data; the reported index is RMSEA.

### H2, caricature

Autism personas are exaggerated and homogeneous. Three predictions, all on AQ-10 binary totals (Allison, Auyeung and Baron-Cohen 2012; cutoff 6; agree-scored items 1, 7, 8, 10). The hypothesis p-value is the maximum of the three (intersection-union). All three must clear the Holm threshold.

- H2a. Simulated Cohen's d (autistic minus no diagnosis) exceeds the human ceiling. The setup-audit benchmark is d = 1.807. The test uses the confirmatory human draws.
- H2b. Simulated autistic SD is below the human autistic SD ceiling. The audit SD is 2.354.
- H2c. The share of simulated autistic people with AQ-10 below 6 is at least 10 percentage points under the confirmatory human share, and below the human ceiling. "Well under" means that 10-point gap. The audit benchmark is 26.61% below 6.

No-diagnosis SD, male vs female Cohen's d, and EQ-10 and SQ-R-10 group differences are reported and are not in the H2 p-value. Condition 4 minus condition 3 on AQ-10 d is a secondary description of the label-only persona.

The E–S D-score is not in the confirmatory tests. Its standardization is unconfirmed in this repo. EQ-10 and SQ-R-10 Cohen's d stand in until the Greenberg et al. (2018) formula is copied from that paper.

### H3, alignment tuning

Instruction-tuned models fit human distributions worse than their base models at matched size.

Pairs: Qwen3 8B, Qwen3 14B, Llama 3.1 8B, Llama 3.1 70B, Gemma 3 12B, Gemma 3 27B. `Qwen3-32B` has no public base and is excluded. The fit is condition-3 mean item JS, averaged over the two groups. The same persona ids are used in both checkpoints. The statistic is the unweighted mean of (JS_instruct − JS_base). Supported when that mean is positive, the Holm-adjusted p < 0.05, and the CI lies entirely above 0.

### H4, scale

Larger models are not reliably more faithful.

Adjacent pairs, same family and same training stage, condition-3 mean item JS:

- Qwen3-14B vs Qwen3-8B, and the same pair of bases
- Qwen3-32B vs Qwen3-14B (post-trained only)
- Llama 3.1 70B vs 8B, instruct and base
- Gemma 3 27B vs 12B, it and pt

Statistic: unweighted mean of (JS_smaller − JS_larger). Positive means the larger model is closer to humans. H4 is supported when the Holm-adjusted one-sided test of "mean > 0" does not reject.

### H5, demographics

Fidelity is worse for smaller groups.

Three condition-3 contrasts, mean item JS, simulated against humans. The hypothesis p-value is the maximum of the three.

| Smaller | Larger | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Sex = Other (code 3) | Sex = Female (code 2) | Female is the larger sex group in the setup audit (272,218 vs 1,702 Other, both primary groups combined). |
| Age 60–90 | Age 18–29 | |
| Region code 12, outside the UK | Region codes 1–11, pooled | Code 13 (other UK) and code 14 are on neither side. |

Within each diagnosis group, N is min(500, eligible in the smaller cell, eligible in the larger cell). A contrast with fewer than 50 eligible profiles in a group uses the other group. A contrast with fewer than 50 in both groups is dropped. If fewer than two contrasts remain, H5 is not estimable and is not counted as supported. The statistic inside a contrast is JS_small − JS_large, averaged over the diagnosis groups that qualify. Supported when the Holm-adjusted intersection-union test rejects and each retained CI lies entirely above 0.

Cells below n = 10 are never sampled and never reported.

## Other reported quantities

Scale scores, complete cases only:

- AQ-10 binary, range 0–10, cutoff 6.
- EQ-10 and SQ-R-10 graded 0/1/2 from Greenberg et al. (2018) PNAS SI Tables S17 and S18, as implemented in `score_items(..., scheme="graded")`.
- SPQ-10 graded 0/1/2/3, all items positively poled (SI Table S19).

For each dataset, model, condition, and group: mean, SD, and the full total-score distribution; the AQ-10 share at or above 6 and the share below 6; the within-group SD ratio, simulated over human.

Item log-probabilities are secondary and open models only. For each persona and item, the four options are shown as A–D in all 24 orders (`scog.infer.permutations.all_orders`). The letter probability is mapped back to the phrase and averaged over orders. Temperature 0, one token. This is not in the hypothesis tests. It separates distributional fit from sampling noise.

The three-seed subset estimates run-to-run variance of the condition-3 AQ-10 group means. It is not a hypothesis test.

## Contamination check

Separate calls, not in the questionnaire context, temperature 0, one call each per model:

1. List the 10 AQ-10 adult items.
2. State the AQ-10 cutoff and the total's range.
3. State which items score a point for agree and which score a point for disagree.

Each answer is coded correct, partial, incorrect, or refused against the key in `short_forms.py`. The report says whether models that know the key could have been playing to the test. This check is not in the Holm family.

## Multiplicity and decision table

One primary p-value per hypothesis, Holm across the five, alpha 0.05. Exploratory, secondary, out-of-distribution, contamination, and per-model results are outside that family.

Each confirmatory hypothesis is reported as: estimate, bootstrap 95% CI, human ceiling 95% interval, Holm-adjusted p, decision. The decision is supported only when the rule stated under that hypothesis holds. Otherwise it is not supported. H5 can instead be not estimable.
