## Checkpoint and selection

A1's draft lists `Qwen/Qwen3-8B-Base` as the ungated public 8B base. Llama 3.1 and Gemma 3 are gated. No A1 result compares base with instruct, so this study trains one checkpoint: `Qwen/Qwen3-8B-Base`. A second variant is not added.

Hyperparameters are chosen on the exploratory split only, after autistic women are removed. Within the remaining exploratory ids, sorted as integers, every id whose position modulo 10 equals 9 is the dev slice (about 10%). The other 90% is the hyperparameter training set. The grid is rank {16, 64} by learning rate {5e-5, 1e-4, 2e-4}, each continued from epoch 1 to epoch 2. The selection score is mean answer-token negative log-likelihood on the first 2,000 sorted dev ids. The winner is the lowest score. Ties break toward fewer epochs, then lower rank, then lower learning rate. The confirmatory run uses that rank, learning rate, and epoch count once.

Item order in the prompt is the Channel 4 column order: `spq_1` to `spq_10`, `eq_1` to `eq_10`, `sqr_1` to `sqr_10`, `aq_1` to `aq_10`. Wording is the four tuples in `src/scog/scoring/short_forms.py`. SPQ-10 column order against the published short form remains unconfirmed (`SPQ10_STATUS`). The same mapping is what the A1 draft uses.

Shuffled examples are the odd positions in the training-id list sorted as integers. The permutation for one id is drawn from a NumPy Generator whose seed is the first 8 bytes of SHA-256 of `20261029:` plus that id, interpreted as an integer modulo 2^63-1. The master seed is 20261029.

MMLU: the `cais/mmlu` test questions, shuffled with seed 20261029, first 200. The subject-and-index list is hashed before the first training chunk starts. Accuracy is scored on the base checkpoint and again after the confirmatory run.

## Research question

If an LLM is fine-tuned on Channel 4 item responses conditioned on demographics and diagnosis, does it become a faithful simulator, and does that fidelity transfer to:

1. new people from the same population (held-out Channel 4 A2 test split);
2. a held-out subgroup never seen in training;
3. a new population and time (YourBrainType, 2024 to 2025, named countries);
4. unseen items (CARD full-length AQ-50, EQ-40 and SQ).

Point 4 is the generalisation claim. Memorising Channel 4 would not transfer to new item wording. Learning how a person with a given profile answers would.

## Hypotheses

Metrics and noise ceilings are the A1 definitions: per-item Jensen-Shannon divergence (log base 2), Wasserstein distance on the 4-point scale, AQ-10 / EQ-10 / SQ-R-10 / SPQ-10 mean, SD and distribution, share above the AQ-10 cutoff of 6, polychoric Frobenius distance, Tucker congruence, CFA of the human factor model, Cohen's d, and the within-group SD ratio. A model is at the human noise ceiling on a metric only when its value falls inside the 95% interval from 1,000 bootstrap draws of two human samples of the same size. Holm correction is across the five primary tests below. Confirmatory tests use the confirmatory and A2-test splits only, after this file is frozen.

- **H1.** On the Channel 4 A2 test set, fine-tuning puts in-distribution fidelity at the human ceiling. Primary test: mean per-item JS and polychoric Frobenius distance are both inside their ceiling intervals. Seen-item comparison with the tabular baseline is reported with H4.
- **H2.** On CARD items that are not in the Channel 4 short forms, fine-tuning lowers mean per-item JS relative to the zero-shot model, and the fine-tuned value stays outside the human ceiling interval.
- **H3.** The A1 caricature shrinks. Once A1 names the zero-shot model and its three caricature numbers (AQ-10 autistic vs no-diagnosis effect size, within-group SD ratio, share of simulated autistic people below the AQ-10 cutoff), each of those three moves toward the human value after fine-tuning. The primary test is a majority of the three moving toward the human value. This hypothesis cannot be scored until A1 gate 2 records those numbers.
- **H4.** On seen Channel 4 items, in the A2 test set, the fine-tuned model has lower mean per-item JS than the conditional tabular model. That is the primary test. Unseen CARD items are the LLM's added value: the tabular model has no item-text features and is not given a score there. The fine-tuned unseen-item JS is the H2 number.
- **H5.** Held-out subgroup fidelity is worse than in-distribution fidelity. Primary test: mean per-item JS on autistic women is higher than mean per-item JS on the A2 test set for the same fine-tuned model, at the same simulated sample size.

## Models and training

Checkpoint: `Qwen/Qwen3-8B-Base`. LoRA on attention and MLP projections (`q_proj`, `k_proj`, `v_proj`, `o_proj`, `gate_proj`, `up_proj`, `down_proj`), bf16, one A100 80 GB. Alpha is twice the rank. Dropout is 0.05. Micro-batch 1, gradient accumulation 8. Ranks in the exploratory grid: 16 and 64. Learning rates: 5e-5, 1e-4, 2e-4. Epochs: 1, then a continuation to 2 from the same checkpoint. Six runs cover the learning-rate by rank grid. The selected triple is then trained once on the confirmatory training set.

Jobs are chained 30-minute chunks with `--signal=B:USR1@180`, checkpoint, and automatic resume. RNG state is saved on CPU. A rerun skips finished work. Missing data, a missing checkpoint, or zero rows stops the job.

General capability: the 200 MMLU questions defined above, scored before the first training chunk and after the confirmatory run.

## Baselines

1. Zero-shot, the A1 condition with demographics plus diagnoses, same item wording. The checkpoint is the A1 model once A1 names it.
2. Few-shot: 10 real training respondents from the same primary group, sex, and age band. If that cell has fewer than 10 training people, the case is counted as skipped and is not filled from a wider cell.
3. Empirical resampling: one real training respondent from that same cell. This is an upper bound for seen items.
4. Conditional tabular model on seen items: per-item multinomial with demographic covariates, plus a Gaussian copula for the covariance. Fit on the training split only.

## Evaluation

For each condition (A2 test people, held-out autistic women, YourBrainType, CARD), generate 2,000 simulated respondents per group with matched real profiles, and compute the A1 metrics against the matching human data and the noise ceilings.

If a condition has fewer than 2,000 real profiles outside the training set, generation stops and the count is reported when it is at least 10. Profiles are not drawn from the training set to fill the gap.

CARD, when it is run: personas from CARD demographics, with a written map from CARD fields to Channel 4 fields. Full-length items in CARD's own response format. Results split into items that also appear in the short forms and items that do not. That map is not written yet.

YourBrainType uses named countries and the 2024 to 2025 window already in the export.
