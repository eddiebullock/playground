# Landscape review: AI assistive technology for autistic adults' social interaction

Prepared 2026-10-24. Scope: prior assistive tech with outcome data; what autistic adults want; reported difficulties; validity and regulation of emotion recognition; gaps. Verification flags: [V] verified against a primary source or abstract; [S] secondary source only (press release, news, blog summary); [U] could not verify / not found.

## 1. Prior assistive technology with outcome data

### 1.1 Stanford Superpower Glass / Autism Glass (children)
- **What:** Google Glass Explorer Edition plus phone app; outward camera, real-time facial expression classification of 8 basic emotions shown as emoji/colour and spoken label; plus "games" (Capture the Smile, Guess the Emotion) for home use. Voss, Wall et al.
- **RCT:** Voss C, et al. "Effect of wearable digital intervention for improving socialization in children with autism spectrum disorder: a randomized clinical trial." *JAMA Pediatrics* 2019;173(5):446-454. doi:10.1001/jamapediatrics.2019.0285. N=71 children aged 6-12 (40 Glass + ABA vs 31 ABA only); 20-min sessions, 4x/week, 6 weeks at home. ITT gain on Vineland (VABS-II) Socialization of 4.58 points vs control (p=.005); SRS-2 and emotion-recognition tests favoured Glass but were not significant. [V/S] https://psychnews.psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.pn.2019.5a14 ; https://www.hcplive.com/view/efficacy-wearable-tech-children-with-asd-
- **Critiques:** Parent-rated primary outcome, parents unblinded, no sham device; 35 of 71 dropped out (per The Transmitter's reading of the paper); treatment group used the device about half the prescribed time; Vineland gains gone six weeks post-treatment; Connie Kasari: autistic children may recognise emotions but struggle to act on them in context (i.e., target may be wrong). Furfaro H, *The Transmitter/Spectrum*, 2 May 2019. [S] https://thetransmitter.org/spectrum/tech-firms-superpower-glass-autism-not-super-experts-say
- **Commercial trajectory:** Exclusively licensed to Cognoa (founder Dennis Wall); FDA Breakthrough Device designation Feb 2019. [S] same source. Cognoa's FDA-authorised product became Canvas Dx (a *diagnostic* aid, 2021), not the glasses therapy; I found no evidence the Glass therapy was ever cleared or marketed [U on reasons]. A mechanism trial (NCT04049981) is registered. https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04049981 ; project page https://autismglass.stanford.edu/
- **Why it did not take off (inference):** children-only, parent-driven, home-only use with family; Google Glass hardware discontinued; small, non-durable, unblinded effects; basic-emotion labelling of others' faces (exactly what Barrett 2019 and autistic critics challenge); business pivot to diagnostics, which had a clearer reimbursement path.

### 1.2 Brain Power "Empowered Brain" / Brain Power Autism System (Ned Sahin)
- Google Glass AR apps (e.g., Face2Face: cartoon overlaid on interlocutor's face to cue eye contact; emotion games), using Affectiva emotion AI. All studies small, company-authored.
- Tolerability: 19/21 (90.5%) children and adults met tolerability criteria. Sahin NT et al. *JMIR mHealth uHealth* 2017;5(9):e140. [V] https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5629347/
- Safety: 16/18 able to wear and use; no major negative effects. Keshav NU et al., *Frontiers in Pediatrics* 2018. [V] https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6111791/
- School feasibility/controlled study: reported improvements on Aberrant Behavior Checklist (social withdrawal, irritability, hyperactivity). [V title/abstract] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6209889/
- Population: mostly children/adolescents. Outcomes are caregiver-rated behaviour, not social participation or wellbeing. Eye-contact cueing is a normalising target directly contested by autistic people (Section 2). No evidence of current commercial availability [U].

### 1.3 MIT Media Lab affective wearables (Picard, el Kaliouby)
- el Kaliouby, Teeters, Picard (2006) "An exploratory social-emotional prosthetic for autism spectrum disorders": wearable camera reading partner's face/head movements in real time (built on the Baron-Cohen Mind Reading taxonomy). [V] https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~julia/papers/kalioubyetal06.pdf
- iSET (Interactive Social-Emotional Toolkit): handheld video capture and live annotation for "just-in-time, in-situ" learning of facial affect; Madsen 2010 MIT thesis compared iSET (n=10) vs Mind Reading DVD (n=10) in young autistic adults; outcome not retrievable ("preliminary results"). [V existence, U results] https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/62661 ; https://iset.media.mit.edu/
- Trajectory: no efficacy publication located [U]. The technology became Affectiva (founded 2009), which pivoted to advertising/market research and automotive and was acquired by Smart Eye in 2021 (from knowledge; not re-verified this session). Lesson: autism was the motivating use case but not the viable market; general emotion-AI markets were.

### 1.4 Microsoft
- Microsoft retired emotion inference in Azure Face (new customers from 21 June 2022, existing by 30 June 2023), citing lack of scientific consensus on the definition of emotions and generalisation problems across use cases, regions and demographics (Natasha Crampton, Responsible AI Standard), but retained the capability internally for accessibility in Seeing AI (for blind users). [S] https://gizmodo.com/microsoft-shutting-down-azure-facial-emotion-recognitio-1849090511 ; https://news.yahoo.com/microsoft-retires-controversial-ai-guess-102901483.html
- Relevance: an industry precedent that "accessibility" is treated as a legitimate carve-out even by a company that withdrew emotion AI on validity grounds.

### 1.5 2024-2026 consumer smart glasses (Meta Ray-Ban, Brilliant Labs, Even Realities)
- Repeated standard and extended searches found **no published study, product or app** using Ray-Ban Meta, Brilliant Labs (Frame/Halo), Even Realities (G1/G2), or Meta Project Aria for autistic social support. [U, absence of evidence] Ray-Ban Meta is discussed as assistive tech for blind/low-vision and intellectual disability users. https://ixd.prattsi.org/2026/02/assistive-technology-ray-ban-meta-ai-glasses/
- Closest research systems: **SocialMind** (Yang et al., arXiv 2412.04036, 2024): LLM-based proactive AR social assistant on glasses, using verbal/nonverbal cues and "implicit personas"; 20 participants (neurotype not reported); 38.3% higher engagement vs baselines; 95% willing to use. [V abstract] https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04036
- Social-acceptability evidence for glasses: Cornell work (Jenny Fu) found bystanders confused by wearer gestures. [S] https://www.newswise.com/articles/new-smart-glasses-incorporate-features-to-address-social-dynamics
- Young adults with/without autism rated smart-glasses remote assistance as usable, autistic group less on ease of use (*Disability and Rehabilitation: Assistive Technology*, 2025). [V abstract] https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17483107.2025.2494660
- The ARC/Emteq project itself is publicly described (neuroaffirmative AI, models handling unclear data). https://www.autismresearchcentre.com/projects/smart-glasses/

### 1.6 Text/LLM tools
- **Goblin Tools** ("Judge" tone analyser, "Formalizer", "Magic ToDo"; OpenAI-backed). iOS rating 4.8 from about 2,700 reviews. **No peer-reviewed evaluation found** of Judge's accuracy or impact with autistic users. [U] https://apps.appfollow.io/ios/goblin-tools/6449003064?country=us ; https://baiccenter.substack.com/cp/200523527
- **Jang JW, Moharana S, Carrington P, Begel A. "It's the only thing I can trust": Envisioning LLM use by autistic workers for communication assistance. CHI 2024.** 11 autistic participants posed their own workplace social questions to a GPT-4 chatbot and a disguised human confederate; most preferred the chatbot, mainly for delivery (direct, bulleted, black-and-white) rather than quality; an autism employment professional judged some LLM advice unhelpful (e.g., "just walk up and start talking" for making friends). [S] https://www.cmu.edu/news/stories/archives/2024/june/people-with-autism-turn-to-chatgpt-for-advice-on-workplace-issues
- **Choi D, Lee S, Kim S-I, Lee K, Yoo HJ, Lee S, Hong H. "Unlock Life with a Chat(GPT)". CHI 2024.** Focus groups/workshops, N=14 autistic individuals (Korea): expected a "non-judgmental space" for everyday and social problems; concerns about critically digesting responses and about disclosing autistic characteristics to the model. [V abstract] https://doi.org/10.1145/3613904.3641989
- **Carik B, Ping K, Ding X, Rho E. "Exploring LLMs through a neurodivergent lens." PACM HCI 9(1), 2025** (CSCW). Reddit, 61 neurodivergent communities: 20 use cases in five themes (emotional wellbeing, mental health, interpersonal communication, learning, productivity); complaints of "overly neurotypical" responses and limits of text-only interaction; community prompt-sharing workarounds; fears of over-reliance and replacing human connection. [V abstract] https://doi.org/10.1145/3701194 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06336
- **Carik et al. CHI 2025** "Reimagining support: autistic individuals' visions for AI in coping with negative self-talk." [V abstract] https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17504
- **Wohn C, ... Rho E. "Are we writing an advice column for Spock here?" CHI 2026.** Audit of 345,000 responses from six LLMs across 12 autism stereotypes: disclosing autism shifted advice towards avoiding social events, confrontation, novelty, romance (press: up to 70% of cases [S]); interviews with 11 autistic participants split between "affirming" and "infantilising"; authors name a "safety-opportunity paradox". [V preprint] https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.12690 ; https://news.vt.edu/articles/2026/04/eng-cs-autism-AI-advice-personalization-or-bias.html
- **Ma R et al. 2026 (arXiv 2601.17946, poster)** "I use ChatGPT to humanize my words": 3,984 posts by self-identified autistic users (Reddit/X/Tumblr, 2023-2025). Affordances: executive offloading, emotional regulation, **translation between autistic and neurotypical communication** (tone, hidden meaning), identity validation. Risks: sycophantic reinforcement of delusional/paranoid thinking, **"automated masking"** replacing authentic voice, conflict with justice values (environmental/data ethics). [V] https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17946v1
- **McNally et al. 2024, *Social Media + Society*:** 25 autistic TikTok creators used ChatGPT to navigate neurotypical environments and to unmask. [S] https://doaj.org/article/f5b4f12baec645d7b53ef131e32831d1
- Also cited in this literature: Hall et al. ASSETS 2025 (human-AI misalignment in LLM job-seeking support for neurodivergent users); Glazko et al. CHI 2025 (neurodivergent GenAI "power users", privacy). [V citation only, via Ma et al.]
- **"Nguyen et al. 2025, Benefits and pitfalls of using AI to assist with social interaction challenges": NOT LOCATED** after standard and extended searches. [U] The nearest match is an anonymous-in-results 2025 SAGE commentary "The use of AI chatbots for autistic people: a double-edged sword" (https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/27546330251370657). Recommend the student supplies the DOI.

### 1.7 LLM social coaching
- **Noora (Stanford; Koegel LK, Lam MS et al.).** RCT: *J Autism Dev Disord* 2025, doi:10.1007/s10803-025-06734-x. N=30 autistic adolescents/adults aged 11-35, immediate vs waitlist; 10 scripted statements/day, 5 days/week, 4 weeks; LLM grades the user's empathetic reply to pre-written scenarios (constrained to limit hallucination). Empathetic responses in a 20-min human video conversation (blind-rated) rose from 16.67% to 50.94%; waitlist near zero change; 67% enjoyed, 94% would recommend; variable adherence. [S] https://www.psypost.org/stanford-scientists-ai-chatbot-helps-autistic-adults-improve-face-to-face-communication-studies-show/ ; https://news.stanford.edu/stories/2025/08/ai-social-coach-noora-autism
- Workplace follow-up: multiple-baseline, n=3 interns aged 19-22, gains to 75-100% empathetic responses with job coaches (*JADD* 2026, doi:10.1007/s10803-026-07313-4). [S]
- Limits: waitlist comparator, developer-run, no long-term follow-up, single target behaviour defined by neurotypical norms. Autistic critique: "the old myth it recycles" (autistic people lack empathy). https://autismanswersback.bearblog.dev/noora-the-ai-that-teaches-autistic-people-empathy-and-the-old-myth-it-recycles/
- **SocialWise (Tang, ICMI 2025; arXiv 2604.15347):** browser-based LLM role-play with RAG over therapy documents; uncontrolled pilot, 34 autistic adults 18-34; helpfulness 4.15/5, 100% would recommend; self-reported benefits: turn-taking 67.6%, conflict problem-solving 52.9%, reduced anxiety 50.0%. Not a trial. [V] https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15347v1
- **NeuroBridge (Haroon et al., arXiv 2509.23434, 2025):** the reverse direction: LLM simulates a direct, literal autistic-style character so *neurotypical* users practise cross-neurotype communication; 12 NT participants reported better understanding; authors warn users readily accepted AI portrayals of autism as accurate. [V] https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23434
- ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06816589 "Using ChatGPT as a Job Coach for Adults With ASD" is registered; details not retrievable. [U] https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06816589

### 1.8 VR / simulation social-skills training
- Meta-analysis (Karami et al., *Frontiers in Psychiatry* 2021): 33 studies, all ages, pooled g=0.74; social/communication g=0.69; emotion/cognitive about 0.45; AR subset (5 studies) g=0.92. Mostly small, uncontrolled; generalisation to real life not established. [V] https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2021.665326/full
- **VR Job Interview Training (Smith MJ et al., *JADD* 2014):** single-blind RCT, autistic adults, VR-JIT n=16 vs TAU n=10; better live role-play interview performance (p=.046); 90% attendance, rated enjoyable. Six-month vocational follow-up published (*JADD* 2015 brief report); job-offer numbers not retrieved [U]. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4772401 . This is one of very few adult-focused, controlled, real-world-anchored simulation results, and it was commercialised (SIMmersion) for interview practice.
- Kandalaft et al. 2013 (VR social cognition training, 8 young adults, Second Life) is commonly cited as pilot evidence (from knowledge, not re-verified).

**Cross-cutting pattern:** Interventions with outcome data are overwhelmingly (a) child-focused, (b) caregiver- or clinician-rated, (c) training a normative behaviour (eye contact, recognising basic emotions, "empathetic statements"), (d) small, short, unblinded, developer-run. None measured autistic-defined outcomes (energy cost, post-interaction recovery, self-efficacy, misunderstanding repair, relationship satisfaction). Tools autistic adults actually adopt (ChatGPT, Goblin Tools) are general-purpose, self-directed, text-based, and have almost no outcome data.

## 2. What autistic adults say they want and do not want

### 2.1 Critiques of autism technology research
- **Spiel K, Frauenberger C, Keyes O, Fitzpatrick G. "Agency of autistic children in technology research - a critical literature review." *ACM TOCHI* 26(6), 2019.** Autism HCI purposes (behaviour analysis, assistive, education, social skills, therapy, wellbeing) embody neurotypical normative expectations framing autism as deficit needing "correction"; autistic children are positioned as secondary to externally defined goals; calls for design that supports appropriation for the users' own goals. [V abstract] https://doi.org/10.1145/3344919 ; https://publik.tuwien.ac.at/files/publik_283237.pdf
- **Williams RM, Gilbert JE. "Perseverations of the academy: a survey of wearable technologies applied to autism intervention." *IJHCS* 143:102485, 2020.** Wearables research fixates on social skills training rooted in popular assumptions about autism; recommends sensorimotor integration, emotional regulation, executive function and communication instead. [V abstract] https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhcs.2020.102485
- **Keyes O. "Automating autism: disability, discourse, and artificial intelligence." *Journal of Sociotechnical Critique* 1(1), 2020.** AI-autism research narratives promote a dehumanising model of autistic people; discursive harms are invisible to conventional AI ethics. [V abstract] https://digitalcommons.odu.edu/sociotechnicalcritique/vol1/iss1/8/
- Sinclair/autistic-community critiques of Noora (above) show that "teaching empathy" framing will be read as deficit framing regardless of efficacy.

### 2.2 Stated preferences (participatory and qualitative)
- Wanted: non-judgmental, always-available, private space; direct structured output; help translating tone and hidden meaning; executive offloading; regulation support (Choi 2024; Jang 2024; Carik 2025; Ma 2026).
- Not wanted / concerns: being steered away from social life by stereotyped advice (Wohn 2026); "overly neurotypical" responses (Carik 2025); automated masking and loss of authentic voice (Ma 2026); over-reliance and replacement of human relationships (Carik 2025); disclosure and privacy (Choi 2024; Glazko 2025); sycophancy reinforcing anxious/paranoid readings (Ma 2026).
- Wearables: focus groups (7 autistic adults, 7 parents) flagged sensory discomfort and visibility/stigma as main barriers, and wanted user control over settings and alerts. [V preprint] https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-7180182/v1
- Autistic adolescents (survey n=73, 10 interviews) gave requirements for emotion-sensing wearables (smartwatch, patch, IR camera). [V abstract] https://doi.org/10.3390/proceedings2191200
- Research priorities: UK community priorities favour practical everyday-life and services research over basic science (Pellicano, Dinsmore, Charman, *Autism* 2014, 18(7):756-770; from knowledge, not re-verified).

### 2.3 Masking / camouflaging costs
- Hull L et al. "Putting on my best normal." *JADD* 2017: camouflaging described as exhausting, followed by anxiety and stress, threats to self-perception. [S] https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10803-017-3166-5 (DOI from knowledge)
- Cassidy S et al. "Risk markers for suicidality in autistic adults." *Molecular Autism* 2018: camouflaging associated with suicidality; causality not established. [S] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6069847 (URL from knowledge)
- Workplace survey (NEXT for AUTISM 2026, n=417): masking and scripting can consume as much energy as the job itself. [S] https://nextforautism.org/updates/national-survey-of-autistic-adults-reveals-what-shapes-their-workplace-experience/
- Design implication: a tool that only helps users perform neurotypical behaviour risks becoming a masking prosthesis, increasing the very cost that predicts burnout and poor mental health.

### 2.4 Double empathy and autistic-autistic communication
- Milton D. "On the ontological status of autism: the 'double empathy problem'." *Disability & Society* 2012;27(6):883-887 (from knowledge).
- Crompton CJ, Ropar D, Evans-Williams CVM, Flynn EG, Fletcher-Watson S. "Autistic peer-to-peer information transfer is highly effective." *Autism* 2020;24(7):1704-1712: 72 adults in 8-person diffusion chains; autistic and NT chains equally accurate; mixed chains degraded faster and had lower rapport. [V] https://nottingham-repository.worktribe.com/output/5141376
- **Important update: the 2025 registered-report replication did NOT reproduce the mixed-chain deficit.** Crompton CJ, Foster SJ, Wilks CEH, Dodd M, Efthimiou TN, Ropar D, Sasson NJ, Lages M, Fletcher-Watson S. "Information transfer within and between autistic and non-autistic people." *Nature Human Behaviour* 2025;9(7):1488-1500. N=311; no difference between single- and mixed-neurotype chains in information transfer; NT chains reported higher rapport; diagnostic disclosure improved rapport. [V] https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nathum/v9y2025i7d10.1038_s41562-025-02163-z.html ; https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/354953 . The student should cite both; the robust finding is about rapport and disclosure, less about information loss.
- Morrison KE, DeBrabander KM, Jones DR, Faso DJ, Ackerman RA, Sasson NJ. *Autism* 2020;24(5):1067-1080: autistic adults reported better rapport and more self-disclosure with autistic partners; observers rated autistic adults lower regardless of partner. [V citation] https://news.utdallas.edu/health-medicine/autism-social-interactions-2020
- Sasson NJ et al. *Scientific Reports* 2017: NT observers formed less favourable first impressions of autistic adults from thin slices, but not from transcripts (content was fine; presentation drove bias). [S] https://news.utdallas.edu/?p=12115 . Diagnostic disclosure and autism knowledge improved impressions (Sasson & Morrison, *Autism* 2019). https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1362361317729526
- Brewer R et al. *Autism Research* 2016;9(2):262-271: autistic facial expressions recognised less well by observers. [V] https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4975602/ ; Sheppard E et al. *JADD* 2016: NT observers struggle to read autistic people's mental states (cited in de Marchena 2025; primary not re-checked). Rifai et al. 2022: rapport predictors differ by neurotype pairing (cited in de Marchena 2025).
- Implication: any model trained on NT expressions will likely misread autistic faces (including the user's own, relevant to Emteq's inward sensing), and the problem is bidirectional, supporting tools that also serve the NT partner or explain NT conventions as conventions rather than correct behaviour.

## 3. Social-interaction difficulties autistic adults report

Prevalence data are sparse; most evidence is qualitative. What exists:

| Difficulty | Evidence | Status |
|---|---|---|

| Negative emotion/anxiety causing word loss or withdrawal | 53% said communication worsens with negative emotion; 94% of coded upset mentions linked to losing words (Miller 2026) | [S] |

"'Anything but the phone!'" *Autism* 2021, doi:10.1177/13623613211014995; ~247 autistic adults; phone last for strangers, email first; reasons: no nonverbal cues, unplanned fast pace, masking pressure | [S] https://behavioristbookclub.com/aba-research/service-delivery/anything-but-the-phone----communication-mode-preferences-in-the-autis/ |

| Post-interaction recovery / rumination | Camouflaging followed by exhaustion and anxiety (Hull 2017); autistic burnout defined by chronic exhaustion, loss of function, reduced stimulus tolerance (Raymaker DM et al., *Autism in Adulthood* 2020;2(2):132-143); interpersonal interaction named as a burnout precursor (Higgins et al. 2021). | Healthcare communication | Barrier to care (Nicolaidis 2015; Raymaker 2017, via de Marchena 2025) | [V via review] |

Also: co-occurring alexithymia (around half of autistic adults) may explain much of the emotion-recognition difference attributed to autism (Bird & Cook, *Translational Psychiatry* 2013; from knowledge), which matters for whether to support self-emotion awareness (interoception) rather than reading others' faces.

**Recommendation:** the feasibility study should collect its own prevalence/priority data (e.g., ranked list of situations with frequency and distress ratings), since none of the above gives population-level frequencies for post-hoc rumination, message-tone ambiguity, or conversation exits.

## 4. Validity of facial emotion recognition and regulation

### 4.1 Science
- Barrett LF, Adolphs R, Marsella S, Martinez AM, Pollak SD. "Emotional expressions reconsidered: challenges to inferring emotion from human facial movements." *Psychological Science in the Public Interest* 2019;20(1):1-68, doi:10.1177/1529100619832930. Facial configurations fail criteria of reliability, specificity, generalisability and validity; the same movement can signal different states by context, person, culture; warns against technologies that assume faces reveal emotions while ignoring context. [V abstract] https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/emotional-expressions-reconsidered-challenges-to-inferring-emotion-from-human-facial-movements.html
- Relevance to the student's framing: complex mental-state recognition from multimodal video (context, speech, prosody) is a different claim from "face to basic emotion", and calibrated deferral directly answers the specificity/context problem. Still, the claim should be "inferring plausible interpretations of what a person may be communicating, with uncertainty", not "detecting emotions".

### 4.2 EU AI Act
- **Art. 5(1)(f)** (applies since 2 Feb 2025) prohibits placing on the market, putting into service or use of AI systems to infer emotions of a natural person in the areas of workplace and education institutions, except where intended for medical or safety reasons. Art. 3(39) defines emotion recognition systems as identifying/inferring emotions or intentions **from biometric data**. Recital 18: excludes physical states (pain, fatigue) and mere detection of readily apparent expressions (a frown, raised voice) unless used to infer emotion.
- Commission Guidelines on prohibited practices, C(2025) 884 final, 4 Feb 2025 (as summarised by FPF): "workplace" read broadly, any physical or virtual work space including home working and hiring; **inferring emotions from written text is outside the prohibition**; customer-directed systems outside it if no employees tracked; medical exception "narrowly interpreted", described as requiring CE-marked medical devices; general wellbeing/stress/burnout monitoring is not "medical"; Guidelines reportedly mention a training-purposes allowance not found in the Act text. [S] https://fpf.org/blog/red-lines-under-eu-ai-act-unpacking-the-prohibition-of-emotion-recognition-in-the-workplace-and-education-institutions/ ; https://www.aigl.blog/ai-act-guidelines-on-prohibited-artificial-intelligence-ai-practices-an-analysis-for-the-emotion-recognition-field/
- Emotion recognition outside the prohibition is **high-risk** under Annex III point 1(c), with Art. 50(3) transparency duties to exposed persons. Digital Omnibus (proposed 19 Nov 2025; provisional deal 7 May 2026; Parliament vote 16 June 2026) delays Annex III obligations to 2 Dec 2027 and Annex I to 2 Aug 2028; no change to Art. 5(1)(f) found. [S] https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/
- **Application to an autism assistive tool (analysis, not legal advice; no guidance specifically on assistive use for autistic or blind users was found [U]):**
  1. A glasses tool that infers a colleague's or teacher's emotions from their face/voice while the autistic user is at work or university plausibly falls within 5(1)(f) unless it qualifies under the medical exception, which per the Guidelines means a CE-marked medical device. A wellbeing/"social coach" positioning would likely not qualify.
  2. Text-only analysis (emails, messages, transcripts) is outside the prohibition and outside the biometric definition, which makes a browser/downloadable text-and-transcript tool the lowest-risk first product.
  3. Inferring the **user's own** state from Emteq biosignals at work is still emotion inference of a natural person in the workplace; the prohibition is not limited to employer deployment by its text, though its rationale is power imbalance [U, unresolved].
  4. Outside work/education (social life, home), the system is high-risk (Annex III), not banned; obligations delayed to Dec 2027.
  5. Framing outputs as interpretations of communicative intent may partly escape "emotion" (the prohibition omits "intentions"), but regulators may treat this as relabelling; do not rely on it.

### 4.3 UK
- No AI Act equivalent; no statutory emotion-recognition ban. UK GDPR applies: biometric data used for identification is special category; emotion analysis may process special category data. ICO (Oct 2022, Deputy Commissioner Stephen Bonner) called emotion analysis "immature... may not work yet, or indeed ever", warned of bias and discrimination, and said no emotion AI yet satisfied data-protection requirements. Bystander data (camera/mic on third parties) is the larger UK GDPR issue for glasses than emotion inference per se.
- A UK-first feasibility study is legally simpler, but a spin-out selling into the EU must design for 5(1)(f) from day one.

## 5. Gaps a 2026 tool could uniquely fill

1. **Adult-focused, self-directed, autistic-defined outcomes.** No trial has measured energy cost, recovery time, self-efficacy, misunderstanding repair, or relationship satisfaction. All wearable trials were children with caregiver-rated outcomes. A feasibility study with autistic-chosen outcomes (co-designed via the existing focus groups) would be novel.
2. **Post-hoc, reflective support rather than real-time prompting.** Every wearable (Superpower Glass, Empowered Brain, SocialMind) intervenes live. No study tests an after-the-fact debrief tool ("what might they have meant; here are 3 readings with confidence; here is how ambiguous this genuinely was") targeting rumination and post-interaction processing, which autistic adults describe as costly. This also sidesteps real-time latency, bystander consent and live-distraction problems.
3. **Calibrated uncertainty and deferral.** No deployed tool expresses ambiguity or abstains. Goblin Tools Judge, ChatGPT and Noora give single confident answers; Ma 2026 shows sycophancy amplifying anxious readings. A tool that says "this is ambiguous to most people too" (grounded in human inter-rater agreement data from EU-Emotions/Mindreading) operationalises Barrett 2019 and double empathy, and is unprecedented.
4. **Bidirectional / double-empathy design.** NeuroBridge trains NT people only; Noora trains autistic people only. Nothing supports both parties or reframes NT conventions as conventions. A "translation" stance (Ma 2026's top use) presented as cross-cultural rather than corrective is untested.
5. **Stereotype-audited personalisation.** Wohn 2026 shows disclosure triggers avoidance advice. A tool evaluated against such audits, letting users set their own goals (approach vs protect), addresses the safety-opportunity paradox.
6. **Text-and-transcript first, multimodal later.** Message/email tone interpretation is a top real-world use with zero controlled evaluation, and text sits outside the EU ban. A browser tool evaluated on accuracy against autistic and NT human raters would be a first.
7. **Consumer-glasses era is empty.** No published autism work on Ray-Ban Meta, Brilliant Labs or Even Realities. Emteq's inward-facing sensing (the user's own affect/arousal, interoception, alexithymia-relevant) rather than outward face-reading of others is unexplored and aligns with Williams & Gilbert's call for emotional-regulation wearables.
8. **Autistic-face validity.** No assistive model has been validated on autistic expressers (Brewer 2016); LLM/VLM performance on autistic faces and voices is untested and a likely failure mode.
9. **Durability and dependence.** No study measures whether gains persist, or whether reliance displaces human support (a top community fear). Build "fade-out" and dependence measures into feasibility.
