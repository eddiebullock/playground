# Second randomised human dataset for CEA: check of 2026-11-09

Purpose: find a second, independent randomised human dataset so that CEA (A4; CFA in frozen files) does not rest on Snoek et al. alone, and read the CALM paper. Scope and rules: metadata, readmes, small code files and article text only were downloaded, to the session scratchpad. No image, stimulus or large archive was downloaded, and nothing was added to the repo except this file. Where a fact could not be checked it says UNVERIFIED.

No public trial-level Bubbles or noise classification-image dataset for emotion categorisation was found beyond Skog et al. (details in Task 2).

Bottom line: Liu et al. 2022 Experiment II is the only candidate that gives a second CEA estimate on the same estimand without new data collection. It is independent in observers and stimuli but not in lab, generator or culture (Western only), so it addresses "one sample" and not "one platform". Skog et al. is independent in every respect but measures a different estimand (a pixel template for surprise detection) and needs material from the authors.

## Task 1: Aston record 596 (Skog, Qian, Parmar and Schofield 2023)

**Citation (checked against the article PDF).** Skog E, Qian CS, Parmar A, Schofield AJ. What surprises the Mona Lisa? The relative importance of the eyes and eyebrows for detecting surprise in briefly presented face stimuli. Vision Research 211, 108275 (2023). DOI 10.1016/j.visres.2023.108275. Article open access under CC BY 4.0. Dataset: Skog, Qian, Parmar, Schofield (2023), Aston University, DOI 10.17036/researchdata.aston.ac.uk.00000596, made available 4 May 2023, grant EP/S016260/1.

**Licence.** CC BY 4.0 for all listed files (record page).

**Files (record page; zip listing read from the archive's central directory only, about 100 KB tail, no member extracted).**

| File | Size | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Experiment 1 Readme.txt | 1 kB | Read in full |
| Experiment1.zip | 16,189,878 bytes | 222 entries: 111 `.txt` and 111 `.tif`, participants P1 to P7 |
| Experiment 2 Readme.txt | 1 kB | Read in full |
| Experiment 2 data.csv | 108 kB | Only the first 3 kB (header) read |

Experiment1.zip holds, per participant, condition and response level (1 to 4), one 128 x 128 accumulated noise image: Female11 28 files of each type, noBrow 28, MonaLisa 27 (P7 never answered 4), Mouth 28 (`.tif`) with `.txt` named `Px_Mouth_y.txt`. The `.txt` files are raw unscaled sums. The `.tif` files are mat2gray-scaled, and the readme says these were the basis of the analysis.

**Observers and trials (article).** Experiment 1: 7 observers, including the four authors, age 20 to 52, run at home in PsychoPy on their own monitors. 2,004 trials per face condition per observer, 18 sessions, so 14,028 noise samples per aggregated CI. A further "attend to mouth" condition with the same observers on the neutral face. Experiment 2: 104 Prolific participants, 3 failed attention checks (101 analysed), 24 images, each rated on seven emotions (happy, afraid, surprise, sad, angry, disgust, neutral) with sliders that had to sum to 100, plus the 33-item SSEIT.

**What is randomised.** Pixels. A new static 128 x 128 white-noise image (4 x 4 deg) on each trial, with the face at 35% and noise at 65% of the luminance contrast. Base faces: KDEF Female 11 neutral; the same face with eyebrows edited out; a digital Mona Lisa. The noise distribution (Gaussian or uniform) and its exact scaling are not stated (UNVERIFIED). Nothing is randomised at the level of regions or AUs. Presentation 500 ms.

**Response format.** "Did the face express surprise?" on four keys: Unlikely, Less unlikely, Less likely, Likely. For the CIs, responses were collapsed to two classes (3 and 4 against 1 and 2), and Full-CI = Positive-CI minus Negative-CI. This is detection of one emotion, not a choice among emotions.

**Per-trial noise or seeds.** Not released. The record holds per-response-level sums only, and the readme says the trial counts behind each sum differ and are not given. No trial-level noise, no seeds, no trial order and no response sequence. The experiment code is not in the record. Consequences: human split-half reliability can be computed only across the 7 observers, not across trials; the human CI cannot be recomputed with another weighting; and the per-level `.txt` sums cannot be turned into means without the unreported trial counts (the scaled `.tif` files remove scale).

**Statistical strength of the human template.** Pixelwise tests (30 x 30 windows, Kolmogorov-Smirnov, moments) found no significant deviation after correction. Significance came only after a Gabor energy filter bank with a Bonferroni threshold of z = 5.4555 and a region count against random noise. The eye region was informative; the mouth region was not, except when observers were told to attend to the mouth. A model comparison would therefore be against a weak, smoothed target.

**Is a model measurement feasible?** Yes in principle, with conditions.

- A model classification image does not need the human noise samples. A CI estimates the observer's template, so the model can be run on fresh noise drawn from the same distribution, and the two templates compared. Matching the human noise exactly would only help a trial-level comparison, which the data do not allow anyway.
- What it does need is the exact stimulus frame: the three base images at 128 x 128 with the same placement, scaling (face height matched), mean and RMS standardisation, and the 35/65 contrast mix, plus the noise distribution. None of these is in the record. The PDF figure is not a usable substitute. Without the exact frame, model and human CIs are misregistered and a pixel correlation is meaningless.
- KDEF is distributed under its own terms (UNVERIFIED for redistribution; the authors may not be able to share the face itself). The Mona Lisa is public domain, so that condition is the easiest to reproduce if the authors share the crop and scaling.
- Readout: Qwen3-VL-8B and Gemma 4 asked the same question with the four options, read as label probabilities; P(Less likely) + P(Likely) gives a graded positive weight per trial, so the CI can be a weighted sum (equivalently, a regression of the readout on noise), which uses more information than the human binary split. Run each model as a 7th or 8th "observer".
- Size: 2,004 trials per condition per human observer; matching the aggregate (14,028 per condition) means about 14,000 forward passes per condition per model, 42,000 for the three main conditions. Images are small. Cost not estimated here (UNVERIFIED), but it is a single-image, four-option prompt.
- Risk: 128 px images upsampled by the vision encoder, and a 500 ms brief-presentation design whose rationale has no model counterpart.

**Mapping to the CEA estimand.** In the theory note's terms, cues z are the 16,384 noise pixels, Q is the white-noise design (Sigma close to sigma^2 I, so lambda_min is at its maximum and Proposition 3's invisibility factor is near 1), and the human CI is, up to scale, the first-order regression of the binary response on z, which under a white design identifies the linear effect tau(j, surprise) per pixel. So it is interventional and fits the CEA logic, with these changes:

1. K = 2 (surprise against not surprise) and one response dimension, so tau is a 1 x 16,384 vector, not options by cues. CEA becomes the cosine (Pearson r) between model and human CI maps. Residual CEA does not carry: with one option row, R removes everything (the same failure as Proposition 2 in the theory note), so only raw CEA is available.
2. Cues are pixels, not AUs. A meaningful comparison needs a fixed low-dimensional basis: the authors' Gabor energy maps (5 frequencies x 24 orientations plus isotropic), or a few regions (brow, eye and mouth strips of 16 x 64 pixels as in the paper), giving a region-level analogue with p of about 3 to a few hundred.
3. One identity per condition, so no generalisation over faces. The A4 V3 check (configuration carries across identities) has no counterpart.
4. Detection with an asymmetric scale, not forced choice among emotions; the paper's own Experiment 2 shows the "not surprise" template reads as disgust, so the negative class is not neutral.
5. Reliability: R_H from split-half over 7 observers only (4 of them authors); R_S over model trials. The ceiling will be low and imprecise.
6. Experiment 2 is not a randomised cue design (each image had one noise sample, and the CI overlays are derived from Experiment 1), so it adds no cue-effect estimate.

Verdict: useful as an independent, method-different demonstration in pixel space (does the model's surprise template sit on the eyes as the human one does?), and as a supplementary analysis. It cannot be a second estimate of A4's confirmatory statistic.

## Task 3: CALM (arXiv 2605.25256)

**Citation.** Weller N, Barkett E. Whose Alignment? Comparing LLM Process Alignment Across Diverse Organizational Decision Contexts. arXiv:2605.25256, 2026. Version read: v2 (4 June 2026), full text, 10 pages; v1 submitted 24 May 2026. Accepted to the Pluralistic Alignment Workshop at ICML 2026. Equal contribution; University of St. Gallen and Columbia University.

**What CALM is.** "Contextualized Alignment Lens Model", a Brunswik lens model or policy-capturing method. For the organisation (historical decisions) and for each LLM condition separately, a ridge-regularised logistic regression of the binary decision on a fixed cue set (one-hot encoded and standardised) gives a coefficient vector beta. Process alignment is cos(beta_org, beta_LLM). Secondary measures: Pearson r of the coefficient vectors, correlation of per-case P(positive), accuracy, Cohen's kappa, ROC AUC. Significance "via bootstrap permutation" (details not given). The ridge strength is not reported.

**Data.** Study 1: 1,000 ECHR Article 6 decisions (balanced), 45 binary cues in nine families, coded from case text by GPT-5.4-mini with a codebook; ten LLMs including Gemma 4 (31B); court ridge regression AUC .714. Study 2: German Credit, 20 cues, a balanced subset of 600 cases (seed 42), five LLMs; bank regression accuracy 71.5%, AUC .776. Each LLM receives "a structured case profile"; whether this is the coded cue vector alone or includes further text is UNVERIFIED from the paper.

**Intervention or randomisation.** No cue is randomised or set. Both betas are fitted on the natural cases with their natural cue correlations. The only manipulations are prompt conditions: "org-externalized" (the organisation's weights described as strong, moderate or weak indicators) and "introspective-externalized" (the model is shown how its own weights diverge). These change the system, not the cue distribution, so they are not interventions on cues in the CEA sense.

**Relation to CEA.**

- CALM is the observational counterpart of CEA: cosine between regression-coefficient matrices on P, where CEA is the cosine between interventional effect matrices on a randomised Q. The cosine form is the same, and CALM is close prior art for "cosine of cue-weight vectors between a model and a human or institutional reference" (it cites Smith et al. 2003 for the same comparative move in policy capturing).
- Proposition 4 applies to the organisation side directly: court and credit decisions depend on case facts not in the coded cues, so beta_org is G-weighted regression slopes, not cue effects. Two decision-makers using different unmeasured facts can share beta. If the LLM sees text beyond the cue vector, the same holds for beta_LLM.
- CALM is a behavioural metric in the theory note's sense (a functional of items and responses on P). So by Theorem 1 and Corollary 1.1, a system matching another on every natural case has the same CALM score while its interventional effects, and its CEA, can take any value.
- Proposition 3 bounds what CALM can recover when systems are linear in the coded cues: with correlated cues (small lambda_min, likely with 45 one-hot legal features and with credit attributes), coefficient differences are poorly identified, and ridge shrinkage blurs them further. Under a linear observer, full-rank cue covariance and no unmeasured cues the system uses, CALM's beta equals the dose effect and CALM coincides with CEA in expectation; none of these conditions is checked in the paper.
- The paper itself says high alignment is "a proxy for equivalent decision processes rather than direct evidence of them" (Limitations), which is consistent with this reading. For the CEA paper, CALM is a citation for the observational version and a foil for why randomisation is needed; it does not claim causal identification.
