# Literature check: VLMs versus randomised human cue effects on faces

**Run:** 2026-11-07. Follows `research/NOVELTY_CHECK.md` (2026-11-04). No repo files edited.
**Read status:** F = read in full. P = full text obtained, only methods or keyword passages read. P* = same, through a summarising fetch tool, so quotes need rechecking. A = abstract only. S = snippet or metadata only. P is added to your three labels because calling these "read in full" would be false. Nothing below is F except short web pages.

## Bottom line

1. **Novelty claim survives.** No VLM has been held to randomised human cue effects on faces in anything searched. Absence of evidence, and Semantic Scholar stayed down.
2. **The claim must be narrowed.** Coarse counterfactual face work on VLMs now exists (Zhang, Chen). Say "cue-level, against randomised human effects", not "causal".
3. **Option-order audit angle is partly taken.** The report's line that "no located emotion VLM paper covers option order" is wrong: Bhattacharyya and Wang 2025 tested it. The combined audit is still open.
4. **Venue:** primary Nature Human Behaviour, fallback TMLR.

General VLM evidence, not emotion: [Li, Qiu and Xu, arXiv 2609.29278](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29278), 93.54% first-slot picks under CoT-prefix scoring on Qwen2.5-VL [A]. [CMMU, arXiv 2401.14011](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.14011) applies CircularEval to MLLMs [P].

**Verdict.** Taken: "option order affects VLM emotion judgements". Open: option position controlled **together with** image mismatch on faces or RMET, and re-estimating published accuracy. Both RMET papers fixed order, and no located emotion paper swaps the face under a fixed prompt. Recast the contribution as "how much published RMET and face accuracy survives joint control", and cite Bhattacharyya and Wang as precedent. Amend `reports/VLM versus human emotion mechanisms.md` line near "no located emotion VLM paper covers option order", and `research_notes/.../prior_ai_human_comparison.md` lines 13 and 187.

**Recommendation (judgement, not fact)**

**Primary: Nature Human Behaviour.** The question is about human and model perception, within its stated scope. Its traditional route is confirmed and is not tied to affiliation, though the absence of page charges is not confirmed. OA is likely covered if a Cambridge author corresponds. Risks: selectivity, slow review against high scoop speed, and a human arm limited to existing avatar data on about 8 of 21 labels. Post a preprint first, after checking its preprint policy, which I did not verify. Submit an abstract to CCN 2027 for a dated record.

**Fallback: TMLR.** Rolling, CC BY, no listed fee, no affiliation needed, and judged on correctness, which suits a rigorous audit plus a new estimand, once its theorem is proved. It loses the psychology audience. Cognition is the alternative if that audience matters more, once its terms are checked.

**Ruled out on fees:** PNAS (unless Jisc is confirmed), Science Advances, Patterns. **ICML 2027:** still too soon, per the report.
