**NOT SUPPORTED AS WORDED (2026-11-08), for the author to resolve; the drafts were not changed.** `methods.md`, the paragraph after the prediction table: "No cited source tests frame shuffling for P12 or P13, and the closest human test found no cost of shuffled or reversed order [50]." In Gold et al. (#50), efficiency, the ratio of ideal to human thresholds, did not differ with shuffled or reversed order. But raw human thresholds were significantly higher for shuffled clips than for static and dynamic ones (Results, Figure 4). That is a raw cost in the direction P12 and P13 predict, which the authors attribute to less physical information in shuffled clips. Reversed order showed neither a threshold nor an efficiency cost. The table entries "Contrary: [50]" for P12 and P13 carry the same issue. Our outcome is a raw label probability, not efficiency, so [50] is contrary evidence only at the level of efficiency. A wording that the source supports: "the closest human test found no loss of efficiency with shuffled or reversed frame order, although shuffled clips raised raw thresholds, which the authors attribute to less available information [50]." Also note that Gold et al. used six basic emotions in noise, not low-intensity or complex labels.
