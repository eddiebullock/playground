# Abstract

Some vision-language models reportedly match people at reading mental states from faces, but several such claims cannot rule out option order or prompt text. In our earlier task, the correct option always came first. We audited three open models on 243 acted clips, rotating option position and swapping faces under fixed prompts. Swapping the face cost all three models accuracy, by 7 to 40 points, though one stayed at chance. We prove that agreement on natural items does not in general determine which cues a system uses. We therefore compared model cue effects with human effects of randomised facial action units, on rendered faces. Gemma 4's cue effects aligned with people's on basic labels, mixed on conversational states; Qwen3-VL's were inconclusive. Stimulus interventions tested preregistered human predictions. Masking or filtering facial regions moved judgements as predicted on some contrasts. Models differ in which degree of human similarity they reach.
