# Figure legends

**Figure 1 | Audit of the earlier task.** **a**, Position of the acted label in the six-option list of the v1 prompt. The target was first on every item, so accuracy equals the share of first-option choices. **b**, Option sets. Each row is an acted label and each filled cell a label offered on its items; None of the above, offered on every item, is not shown. Each label had one option set, so the options identify the category without the image. Excitement is the workbook's spelling on Kind items and is kept as written. **c**, Delivery. Median image tokens across the face, over the 243 clips, per input route and model. Dashed line, the gate of 8 tokens. Filled markers pass all four delivery gates; open markers fail at least one. On Gemma 4 the composite grid (`legacy_full`) leaves about 1 token across the face. **d**, Weak labels. Mean share of face-only human observers choosing the acted label, per label, from the item norms. Orange, the three flagged weak labels (Kind, Unfriendly, Jealous). Dashed line, chance at 1/6. **e**, The eight published VLM emotion designs we examined [1,2,8-13], as the repository's reading records them. Columns: whether target position was controlled (randomised: yes; grouped fixed orders compared: partly; fixed or not randomised: no), whether one option set served all items so that the prompt cannot identify the category, whether delivery of the image to the model was checked, and whether face dependence was tested (an estimate of accuracy lost when the face is swapped under a fixed prompt: yes; an inversion arm, a no-image control or a fixed-text neutral twin: partly). [1] is coded no on option sets: its alternatives were set per item, and on the MRMET a repeated label always came with the same three alternatives, the fault shown in **b**. "Does not arise" marks designs with no option list in the main readout; "not stated" means our reading, at the level given per row, found no such statement; "inferred" marks a cell that rests on an inference recorded in the repository. No paper reached yes on face dependence. Source lines and reading status per row are in `research/reproduction/literature_table.csv`. Inputs: `prompt_confound_audit.json`, `input_delivery_{gemma4,qwen3vl,molmo2}.json`, `data/eu_emotions_human_entropy.json`, `literature_table.csv`. No model response is plotted.

**Figure 3 | Behaviour on natural items does not in general settle cue use.** **a**, Proposition 2. Observer S responds to cue z1 only and observer T to cue z2 only. When the two cues correlate with rho on natural clips, the two outputs correlate with rho, but their cue effects are orthogonal, so CEA is 0. **b**, The same case computed: output correlation and CEA between S and T at rho of .5, .9 and .99. Behaviour converges while CEA stays at 0. **c**, Theorem 1 computed. Systems built to match a fixed system on every natural item reach any requested CEA with it. Their natural-item outputs equal the fixed system's by construction; the number shown is the largest error of each constructed effect matrix against the requested one. Grey line, identity. **d**, Smallest eigenvalue of the cue correlation matrix per cue table, log scale. Blue, detector cues on the 243 EU-Emotion clips (LibreFace, OpenFace 3.0). Orange and green, the randomised Snoek designs with action units as coded and with left and right versions merged. Dashed line, 1, the value for uncorrelated cues. By Proposition 3, 1 / lambda_min bounds how far behavioural agreement can understate a difference in cue effects. **e**, Condition number of the same matrices. Natural clips are far less well conditioned than the randomised designs, and most of the Snoek correlation comes from bilateral pairs. Input: `research/reproduction/cfa_identifiability.json`; theory in `research/theory/causal_feature_alignment.md`.
