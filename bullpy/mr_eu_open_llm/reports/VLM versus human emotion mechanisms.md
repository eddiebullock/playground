# Rebuild the study around causal cues

The study should stop asking whether models and humans reach the same answers and instead test whether the same facial cues, when intervened on, move both systems the same way, through a model path that a cut can remove. No similarity claim is currently available, because the 2026-11-04 audit showed that 6AFC accuracy and last-token probes on the frozen task cannot separate perception from option position and prompt text, so RQ0 (amendment A1) is the first result that counts. The agreed RQ0 to RQ5 already map onto all five supervisor questions, but the human-mechanisms review changes the plan in five places: head angle should leave the happy-to-sad test, playback speed should enter it, Kind, Unfriendly and Jealous should stop being scored as ground truth from faces, RQ5 needs new pairs, and the current 1 fps pipeline cannot deliver the kinematic cue to the model at all. The largest constraint is on the human side: the only free randomised human cue data inspected so far (Snoek) covers action units for about 8 of the 21 EU-Emotion labels, with no gaze, head or timing, so RQ1 and RQ2 can only partly make human-referenced claims without a new human arm. Genuine novelty remains in linking randomised human cue effects to interventional model cue effects, because "mechanistic interpretability of VLM emotion" as a bare topic is already taken at ICML 2026. That link fits a general estimand, Causal Feature Alignment, but its theorem is unproved, and the realistic targets are CCN in February 2027 and NeurIPS 2027 rather than ICML 2027. All null-literature statements below rest on one day of metadata searching and need checking before any priority claim.

## No similarity claim survives until RQ0 passes

Tags used throughout. Evidence strength follows the notes: **[strong]**, **[moderate]**, **[contested]**, **[thin]**. Publication status: **[PR]** peer-reviewed, **[accepted]** accepted per an arXiv comment and not checked in proceedings, **[PP]** preprint. Human-literature papers are PR and read at abstract level unless marked; only O'Reilly et al. was read in full, through a summarising fetch. **[snippet]** and **[secondary]** mark weaker reading. Links to arXiv with no venue recorded in the notes count as PP. **[project]** means a local file, **[computed]** means I calculated it from local data for this report, and **[inference]** marks reasoning rather than a finding.

The study's positive evidence has mostly gone. The target sat in option 1 on 243 of 243 items, one option set existed per label, category alone gave grouped T1 rho = 0.503 against peak probes of 0.471 to 0.518, and last-token activations decoded category at 0.96 to 1.00 against accuracy of 0.20 to 0.38 ([DECISIONS 2026-11-04](../research/DECISIONS.md)) [project]. What remains is thin: shuffled 4AFC runs (n = 118) above chance for Qwen2-VL (0.415) and Gemma 4 (0.432), and small, uncorrected within-category links to human p_target (r = 0.16 to 0.21) [project]. The literature has shown the same trap elsewhere. In one VLM preprint, Qwen2.5-VL-7B falls from 80.76% to 45.48% under CoT-prefix scoring and 93.54% of predictions pick the first slot ([Li et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29278)) [PP, abstract]; on GPQA Diamond, the correct-first group outperformed all others ([Zhu et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.04734)) [PP, snippet]; and steering effects can follow answer identifiers rather than meaning ([Gao et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.22985)) [PP].

"How similar" should be answered as a profile, not a score. Schyns, Snoek and Daube's three degrees (same behaviour, same features, same computation) are the frame RESEARCH_QUESTIONS.md already adopts ([TiCS 2022](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/278852)) [PR, snippet]. Each step needs a human noise ceiling, since CogGym's best models reached R² of 0.58 on images against human split-half of 0.95 ([arXiv 2609.21259](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21259)) [PP, abstract; a project page gives lower figures, unresolved]. Item-level comparison is the informative level: models match primates at object level but not image level ([Rajalingham et al.](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/38/33/7255)) [PR, snippet], and Asanuma et al. found item-level matching far weaker than category-level RSA in VLMs ([arXiv 2505.12746](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12746)) [PP]. Model-recovery checks belong in the claim too, because recovery of the generating model stayed below 80% even with millions of simulated trials ([Avitan and Golan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.23321)) [NeurIPS 2025, PR per arXiv listing, abstract].

| Supervisor question | RQ | Causal test | Free human reference | Blocking issue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| How similar are they? | Overarching; RQ0 gates it | A1: real versus mismatch across 5 rotations | O'Reilly option distributions, unedited clips | 30 runs pending; A1 does not fix category-identifying option sets |
| What image information drives humans? | RQ1 | Cue-by-option effect matrix | Snoek, AUs only | Covers about 8 of 21 labels |
| Is it encoded in models? | RQ1, RQ3 | Within-pair contrast at image tokens, then a cut | Not needed; model-only | Decoding shows presence, not use |
| Does intervening change judgement? | RQ2 | Graded edit versus zero-delta edit | Snoek amplitude curves for AUs only | No human curve exists on edited EU-Emotion clips |
| Do they do it a very different way? | RQ4, RQ5 | Image-free baselines, foil swap, option-free readout | RMET and MRMET selections; O'Reilly | Needs a working blind readout |
| Is it causal? | RQ2, RQ3 | Necessity and sufficiency patches, joint knockouts | Human side is identified only by randomisation | Model-specific cut-points untested |

RQ0 needs one addition that A1 cannot supply. A1 is a 6AFC test, frozen before data, and should stay as it is. Later RQs use a blind, option-free readout, so a model can pass A1 and still carry too little image signal on that readout, a risk the causal notes call the biggest in the plan (gates G4 and G5) [inference]. The fix is a second, separately preregistered validity gate on the blind prompt before RQ1, not a change to A1. A1 itself leaves defect 2 open, because label sets still identify category ([PREREGISTRATION](../research/PREREGISTRATION.md)) [project].

## Human evidence rewrites the cue list

RESEARCH_QUESTIONS.md was written before the human review finished and lists "action units, gaze, head angle, dynamics", with happy to sad as the worked example. Most of that list survives. Its weights and its example do not.

**The happy-to-sad test needs other levers.** The review's inference names speed, mouth composition and context as human-evidenced levers, and its own adaptation section adds a fourth: exposure to a sad face pushes an ambiguous face towards happy ([Rutherford et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-1523.34.1.27)) [strong, basic]. Same-actor morph continua add a direct estimate of where each system puts the boundary ([Calder et al. 1996](https://doi.org/10.1080/713756735)) [strong, basic]. RQ2's wording can stand; only its example should change. The notes' prediction that a pose-driven flip marks non-human dependence needs a caution: the evidence is an absent study, not a demonstrated human null. Mignault and Chaudhuri 2003 and Hess et al. 2007 are the obvious sources, and neither abstract was retrievable. A model flip should be reported as "no human precedent located", not as a failure [inference].

**The pipeline cannot currently deliver speed.** Sampling takes `min(max_frames, floor(duration × 1 fps))` frames uniformly across the whole clip, and the default presentation is a composite grid ([frame_sampling.py](../scripts/frame_sampling.py), [config.py](../config.py)) [project; read, not run]. Any clip long enough to hit the cap at every speed gets the same frame positions at 0.5x, 1x and 2x, so the model is blind to speed by construction. With the cap of 4 used for the earlier extract, that is every clip of 8 s or more, and at the config default of 16 it is every clip of 32 s or more [computed]. Shorter clips change frame count, and so grid layout, with speed. Frames about a second apart also cannot carry onset velocity, while the human integration window for conversational expressions is at least 100 ms ([Cunningham and Wallraven 2009](https://doi.org/10.1167/9.13.7)). A null on the current pipeline would measure the pipeline. The speed test needs native video at a fixed, high frame rate and fixed frame count, with sent indices logged, and it inherits Qwen's temporal patch merging two frames per token ([config](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct/raw/main/config.json)). The same limit applies to the whole "dynamics" entry in RQ1: reversal and truncation only test temporal order if order reaches the model. Spatial resolution has a parallel limit, at about 4 by 4 tokens per face on a 4 by 4 grid in Gemma, so a lip corner can be sub-token and AU tests need single-frame input ([causal notes](../research_notes/VLM%20versus%20human%20emotion%20mechanisms/causal_methods.md)) [computed there].

**Local data confirm the label-reliability problem and sharpen it.** Applying (p − 1/6)/(5/6) to `data/eu_emotions_human_entropy.json` reproduces the paper's face mean (62.7% against 63%) and Joking (90.1% against 90%), and gives Kind 7.5% and Unfriendly 8.9% [computed]. The formula is my assumption, justified by that match, and the local file holds 243 of the paper's 249 face clips. It gives Jealous 10.9% against the paper's 13 to 14%. For these labels humans do not merely score low; their modal answer is usually a different word.

| Label | Items | Human p(target) | Top foil and mass | Items with modal answer not the target |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kind | 9 | 0.229 | Interested, 0.42 | 7 |
| Unfriendly | 10 | 0.241 | Bored, 0.46 | 8 |
| Jealous | 7 | 0.257 | Disappointed, 0.43 | 5 |

These 26 items hold 20 of the 29 items in the whole set where the human mode is not the target [computed]. A model that answers Bored on an Unfriendly clip agrees with people and scores as wrong, while a model scoring far above humans here should be suspected of leakage from actor, framing or text, as the review predicts. So the primary alignment metric should be a noise-ceiling-normalised divergence from the human option distribution, with accuracy secondary. A chance-level model on these labels may be human-like.

The same data close one gap and correct one inference in the review. Its per-label figures for Bored, Worried, Interested and Disappointed were missing; locally they are 68.1%, 63.1%, 64.9% and 54.7% chance-corrected, so all four are reasonably face-readable [computed]. Its proposed "low-face, high-body" class listed Disappointed and Frustrated beside Kind and Unfriendly. Disappointed (54.7%) and Frustrated (73.7%) do not belong there: body scenes score higher, but the face is not near floor [computed]. Only Kind, Unfriendly and Jealous sit at floor. Disgusted (item range 0.01 to 0.96) and Angry (0.14 to 0.91) also contain individual failed items, so filtering should be per item, not only per label.

Two further limits apply to the RQ1 test as written. The Snoek files contain 33 AU amplitude columns plus face, participant and label fields, and no pose, gaze or timing, as confirmed for one participant file [computed; theory notes report the same across the set] ([dataset](https://doi.org/10.21942/uva.21261885)). RQ1's human matrix can therefore cover AUs only. Gaze, head and dynamics have to be compared against literature direction, or against new data. Snoek's labels are six basic emotions plus bored, interested, confused and thinking, which overlap about 8 of the 21 EU-Emotion labels and include none of Worried, Disappointed, Kind or Unfriendly [inference from label lists]. AU measurement is also uneven just where the happy-to-sad example lands: LibreFace intensity correlates .85 for AU12 and .78 for AU4 but only .36 for AU15 and .34 for AU20 ([LibreFace](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.10713v2)) [PP], so the AU1+4+15 sad cluster needs parametric ground truth rather than a detector.

## Causal claims need a ladder, not a stronger probe

"Encoded" and "used" are different claims, and the audit is the project's own proof. A decoder shows information is present. Only intervention shows use, and a positive probe at a prompt-contaminated site shows neither. The honest structure has four rungs, each with its own failure condition.

**Rung 1: fix the measurement.** Blind prefill is the preferred readout: fixed text, no labels, logprobs read over a preregistered lexicon, so only the image can drive item differences. Single-probe yes-or-no comes next, then Latin-square MCQ as a bridge to existing results, then free response on a subset. If the image precedes the text in a causal model, image-token activations should not change with the option list, and that is a unit test rather than an assumption; Gemma's bidirectional handling needs checking. The gates are cheap, about 15 to 30 GPU-hours on project planning figures: timing, invariance, self-patch gives exactly zero change, all-vision cut reproduces the image-free output, and token-level image swap gives a monotonic effect ([causal notes](../research_notes/VLM%20versus%20human%20emotion%20mechanisms/causal_methods.md)) [inference; forward-pass cost unmeasured]. Prefill brings its own risks: low signal, label-prior bias across lexicon entries, and a shift from recognition to completion.

**Rung 2: intervene on the stimulus (RQ1, RQ2).** Start with interventions that need no generator, as the review argues: masks, eye-only crops, filters, reversal, truncation, speed. They require no sham and they carry the highest-ranked human evidence. Occluder choice is itself an intervention, so fill a region from the actor's neutral frame and compare at least two fills. Region Shapley over 8 regions needs 256 coalitions, or 30,720 forwards for 40 items across 3 models, about 9 to 26 GPU-hours assuming 1 to 3 s per forward [computed in notes]. Pixel-space Bubbles on one model is 17 to 50 GPU-hours and should stop at split-half plateau, not at a borrowed human trial count. No application of Bubbles to a generative VLM was located.

Generative edits come next, with a two-arm design. No located tool gives verified, AU-level, identity-preserving editing of real video. The parametric arm uses FACSHuman, where AU values are known by construction ([Gilbert et al.](https://link.springer.com/10.3758/s13428-021-01559-9)) but which is a v0.0.1 alpha whose validation effect sizes went unread. The photoreal arm uses LivePortrait or per-frame MagicFace ([LivePortrait](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.03168), [MagicFace](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.02260)) [PP]. LivePortrait exposes keypoints rather than AUs, so an AU mapping must be learned; no published AU-level evaluation of it was found. The primary contrast is `y(edit_k(x)) − y(edit_0(x))`, never against the original, because the latter measures the generator. Effects count only if bidirectional where possible, graded across doses, verified by two detector lineages, and replicated across generators. Grid-based stimuli and flicker from per-frame editors remain live threats. Humans are not exempt from artefact: faces presumed to be deepfakes gave dampened responses for smiles but not angry expressions ([Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-42802-x.pdf)) [PR, snippet].

The human side of RQ2 is where the study is most exposed. Every human prediction in the review comes from other stimuli, and it names no baseline measured on EU-Emotion. A Prolific run of 60 participants for 20 minutes costs about £240 ([pricing](https://www.prolific.com/pricing)), which the no-cash rule excludes; a free departmental pool is unverified, ethics approval would be needed, and whether EU-Emotion's licence allows edits is unknown. Snoek offers a partial substitute. AU amplitudes were randomised, so P(label | AU amplitude) gives a free human dose-response for AUs, identified without mediation assumptions [inference]. That curve comes from Glasgow generative faces, not from EU-Emotion actors, and FACSHuman amplitude units may not map onto it, so comparison should be at rank or monotone-link level and domain shift could pass for a strategy difference. Without a human arm, RQ2 becomes a causal statement about the model, compared with humans on Snoek AUs and on literature direction, and the paper must say so.

**Rung 3: intervene inside the model (RQ3).** The 5/180 null says nothing about vision: it intervened on a last-token axis that carries option text, and nulls have many other explanations, including backups ([IOI](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.00593.pdf)) [PP], self-repair ([Rushing and Nanda](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15390)) [PP], overdetermination ([Mueller 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04690)) [PP] and interactions ([Vaidyanathan et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27510)) [PP]. A naive vision cut also misses architecture-specific routes, read from configs and code but not run. Qwen3-VL-8B injects ViT layers 8, 16 and 24 into the language model through DeepStack, so patching only `inputs_embeds` leaves three paths live ([code](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/huggingface/transformers/main/src/transformers/models/qwen3_vl/modeling_qwen3_vl.py)). Gemma 4 E4B adds a per-layer signal derived from inputs at every decoder layer ([code](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/huggingface/transformers/main/src/transformers/models/gemma4/modeling_gemma4.py)). Molmo2 reads ViT layers −3 and −9 ([code](https://huggingface.co/allenai/Molmo2-O-7B/raw/main/modeling_molmo2.py)). Results cannot be pooled across models.

The causal notes set a seven-part standard for mediation by cue C through representation R: C changes the answer; C changes R within pair; patching R from the cue-off clip reduces the effect; patching R from the cue-on clip restores it; the patch stays near-manifold; a control of similar norm does nothing; and the result replicates in a second generator [inference]. No located paper meets all seven in a VLM. Reporting necessity and sufficiency separately exposes the interaction term a one-way patch hides. Search should run top-down, from complete cuts through layer bisection to region tokens and subspaces. Joint knockouts test redundancy between mouth and eye tokens and between DeepStack depths. Region-token work has a known limit: on LLaVA, blocking object tokens gave a 33% drop and object plus one buffer gave 72%, so information leaks past the footprint ([Neo et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.07149v2)) [ICLR 2025, PR], and one preprint reports near-zero correlation between causal contribution and spatial overlap ([Where To Look?](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.10758v1)) [PP, undefined metrics, no code]. LEACE at image positions, fitted on within-pair differences from training actors, is a good second instrument ([paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.03819)) [PP]; it is linear, so a nonlinear decoder must confirm erasure. The notes recommend skipping SAEs, steering as a primary test, and feature visualisation on this timeline.

**Rung 4: decide what a result means.** The primary outcome is within-item change in logit difference, modelled per model with actor random effects and inferred by actor-level sign-flip or cluster bootstrap. Flips are secondary, reported against a measured nuisance rate. A null counts only if gates passed, dose was shown effective elsewhere, joint ablation was also null, equivalence passed against a pre-set margin, and divergence was reported. Otherwise the result is inconclusive. The Holm floor lesson carries forward: `k/1001` gave .036, .042 and .032, so the old gate measured permutation count, and it stays locked as a labelled post hoc diagnosis [computed in notes, confirmed by PREREGISTRATION]. Step-down maxT avoids that floor in new work. Actor count sets a separate limit: 17 actors split into discovery and confirmation gives about 8 per arm, with an exact two-sided floor of .0078, and Jealous, with 7 actors by my parse of stimulus IDs, floors at .0156, so it cannot pass α = .01 alone [computed]. With this few actors, claims stop at this stimulus set.

## "A very different way" is a measurable prior-versus-evidence split

RQ4 should be the study's sharpest behavioural claim, because the human and model constraints run in opposite directions. RMET performance in people loads on vocabulary and emotion perception: Peterson and Miller found verbal IQ, not face perception, explained variance in 42 students ([2012](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2012.00220)); the short form correlated with vocabulary at .49 and .62 ([Olderbak et al.](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.01503/full)); and a meta-analysis gave r = .33 for emotion perception, .25 for vocabulary and concluded the test acts as an emotion perception measure ([Kittel et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1177/1073191121996469)) [strong that it is contaminated; contested on what remains]. Validity doubts are serious: nearly a quarter of items failed the original inclusion criterion in a large sample ([Higgins et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1177/10731911221124342)). A language model has near-perfect vocabulary, so a superhuman RMET score could reflect the test's lexical structure, and text models can predict sensory judgements with no vision at all ([Marjieh et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01308)) [PP]. Exposure is a further threat; Strachan et al. flag that faces and options may sit in training data ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.22309)).

The review's three-route taxonomy gives RQ4 a usable shape, provided it is labelled as a synthesis rather than an established model: R1 signal reading (AU and motion to label), R2 appraisal and context, R3 conceptual and verbal. The hypothesis is that VLMs overweight R3 and underweight R1 dynamics [inference]. Three tests split it. First, an image-free and mismatch baseline with a fitted decomposition. Barua et al.'s `sJ = βT·eT + βV·eV + π0` is the most reusable template, with R² of about 0.69 to 0.86 on 2,765 conflict items, but it has no humans and no emotion ([arXiv 2610.19407](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.19407)) [PP], and Liu et al. supply a vision-versus-language sensitivity metric on depth cues ([ECCV 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01503)) [accepted, abstract]. Second, foil swaps on RMET, using matched-frequency or synonym foils with the image held fixed, plus an item-difficulty correlation partialling word frequency. MRMET serves as an open replication, though its images differ, so it is not a paired test. Third, regress each model's confusions on its own text-embedding similarity among the 21 labels, using `data/human_confusion_rdm.npy` and `data/eu_emotion_definitions.json`. Human concepts also shape perception ([Brooks and Freeman 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-018-0376-6)) [moderate, basic], so "semantic" is not automatically "non-human". The comparison is of balance, and the human reference for it has to come from an experiment.

Reliability weighting is the cleanest computational contrast. Ong et al. treat attribution as Bayesian cue integration ([Cognition 2015](https://cascoglab.psy.utexas.edu/desmond/pubs/2015_Affective_Cognition.pdf)) [PR, snippet], though a naturalistic test found mixed results, with situation alone often beating the integration model ([Nature Communications 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s41467-024-46670-5)) [PR, snippet]. Vary face reliability, cross it with stated context, and fit context weight as a function of reliability. Human precedent suggests a rising curve: gaze, motion, context and words all matter more when signal is poor, though reading this as one arbitration law is an inference. A flat curve means fixed integration, and text dominance is the likely failure given Deng et al.'s "blind faith in text" ([CVPR 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Deng_Words_or_Vision_Do_Vision-Language_Models_Have_Blind_Faith_in_CVPR_2025_paper.html)) [PR, snippet]. The notes' CREMA-D proposal anchors this cheaply in human data, since per-clip votes exist for voice-only, face-only and audiovisual conditions ([repo](https://github.com/CheyneyComputerScience/CREMA-D)), but its six labels are basic and whether open VLMs take audio natively is unchecked.

Inversion, RQ4's holistic extension, has a modest human baseline. Costs are small and emotion-specific, with happy nearly spared and sad read as neutral ([McKelvie 1995](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2044-8309.1995.tb01067.x)), and processing is mixed rather than purely holistic ([Calder et al. 2000](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-1523.26.2.527); [Fiorentini and Viviani 2009](https://doi.org/10.1080/13506280701821019)) [moderate to strong, basic]. No located composite, inversion or Thatcher study covers complex states, so the test runs first on the basic labels, and Strachan's GPT-4o inversion shift is the only VLM precedent, with fixed options and a closed model. A vision abstract reports a smaller inversion loss than humans ([JOV](https://jov.arvojournals.org/article.aspx?articleid=2802000)) [snippet], which sits awkwardly with Strachan's finding that GPT-4o fell below humans; the two need reconciling. Patching can then locate where an inversion cost arises, which no located paper does.

**RQ5 must be rebuilt again, because its pairs were partly label artefacts.** The two pairs that led the old axes, Bored–Unfriendly and Interested–Kind, are the review's floor labels seen from another angle. Unfriendly to Bored (0.46) and Kind to Interested (0.42) outweigh the target itself, and both run one way only: Unfriendly is not offered on Bored items, and Kind is not offered on Interested items [computed]. DECISIONS raised this for Bored–Unfriendly and left shared prompt text as a live explanation; the Interested–Kind asymmetry is new here. The human explanation may be that actors' intended Unfriendly reads as bored from the face, which is not a confusion at all but a correct percept against an unreliable label [inference]. Across the set, only 12 of 71 offered label pairs are reciprocal, with label variants such as Excitement and Excited left unmerged [computed]. Disappointed–Worried is the only reciprocal pair with substantial mass in both directions (0.14 and 0.21) and is the natural primary target. Kind–Proud (0.21, 0.03) and Disgusted–Sad (0.12, 0.02) are weaker. RQ5's "options crossed with images" can be run freely on models, but human responses exist only for the fixed sets, so a full human crossing requires new data. Candidate explanations for any shared confusion should be tested as a factorial on image, option set and readout, and a positive result means the intervention moved the named pair and not others. No located intervention shifts one specific human confusion pair.

Two guardrails complete the picture. Models have no motor system, and Moebius evidence rules out mimicry as necessary for recognition while modulatory roles remain open ([Rives Bogart and Matsumoto 2010](https://doi.org/10.1080/17470910903395692); [Holland et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1080/02699931.2020.1815655)) [contested], so "non-embodied, therefore different" is not a claim the study can make. Lesions test a computation, never a person: state rival lesions and dose curves in advance, score item-level specificity, run recovery checks, and treat persona prompts as a stereotype control, never as a lesion ([theory notes](../research_notes/VLM%20versus%20human%20emotion%20mechanisms/theory_data_venues.md)) [inference; no published standard located]. EU-Emotion results should be written as recognition of acted conventions, since felt-state inference is weak ([Barrett et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100619832930)) [strong, basic].

## Novelty sits in the human-to-model causal link

The occupied ground is clear. Accuracy on RMET and posed faces is answered ([Strachan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.22309), PP, with a Computers in Human Behavior listing seen only on a portal; [Refoua et al. 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-39292-y), PR, abstract). Confusion similarity is partly answered, and a CNN showed human-like confusion and categorical perception from face experience alone four years ago ([Zhou et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abj4383)) [PR, abstract]. Mechanistic VLM emotion work has appeared three times since May 2026: Zhang et al. on Qwen3-VL ([ICML 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.21980)) [accepted, read to char 100,000], Zhao et al. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17102)) [PP] and CMES ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34742)) [PP]. All three lack human perceptual comparison, and Zhao et al. concede that late-layer effects may be answer selection. Zhao, Wang, Liu and Tang decompose direct and indirect effects by layer for gender cues ([EMNLP 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16651)) [accepted, abstract], so the method is taken while the facial-cue version is not.

The empty cell is Degree 2 and Degree 3 for VLMs on faces: no located VLM classification image or AU tuning curve, no located study treating a facial cue as the mediated variable, and no located paper linking mechanistic VLM methods to human participants. That null comes from arXiv metadata and web search in a single day, with Google Scholar and Semantic Scholar unavailable, so CCN, VSS and CogSci 2026 abstracts and MindSet's ViT results must be checked first. Scoop speed is high: Wen et al.'s counterfactual object ablation with 16,603 human responses is one domain away ([NeurIPS 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13047)) [accepted, abstract].

Reviewers at main tracks will want a general claim. Accepted papers tend to offer methods or measurement results with human data as test bed, such as error consistency ([Geirhos et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.16736.pdf)) and system-identification limits ([Han et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/han23d.html)). ICML asks for work "of significant interest to the machine learning community" with reproducible experiments or sound theory ([ICML 2026 call](https://icml.cc/Conferences/2026/CallForPapers)). Causal Feature Alignment fits this: the estimand is the vector of average causal effects of cue j on option k, from randomised human stimuli and from model interventions, scored against a noise ceiling. The core non-identifiability result looks straightforward. If cues A and B are perfectly correlated on natural items, a system using A and a system using B give identical answers, so error consistency is 1, yet they differ arbitrarily under interventions that decouple A and B [inference, a sketch and not the finite-sample theorem]. The harder parts are disattenuation bounds, recovery rates against the Avitan and Golan limit, and a demonstration that conclusions change against existing metrics. Near-synonyms of "mechanistic alignment" abound, so novelty needs a targeted check. Teneggi and Sulam's concept test with error control is the closest precedent found ([NeurIPS 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19146v2)) [snippet].

Snoek should be the centre of the general paper and EU-Emotion its validation. It is CC BY 4.0 with trial-level data and built-in splits, whereas EU-Emotion cannot be redistributed and NeurIPS E&D requires data reachable without personal request ([call](https://neurips.cc/Conferences/2026/CallForEvaluationsDatasets)). The obvious objection is that avatars lack skin-folding detail and ecological validity, and that the question concerns naturalistic mental states. That objection is correct, and it is why both arms are needed: Snoek identifies the human cue effects, EU-Emotion and RMET test whether they hold on the target task, and rendering is the weak link. Forward use presents human-tested AU vectors to the model. Reverse use runs reverse correlation on the VLM, needing only render time. Availability of Glasgow's generative renders is unverified, and an early request to Jack's group decides how strong this arm can be. No public dataset located combines complex states, video, option-level human distributions and AU coding, so an honestly released derived resource could be a contribution of its own.

Three outputs in sequence fit the evidence. The first is a cross-encoding audit of VLM emotion claims, combining A1 with the delivery audit above (frame rate, token resolution, label reliability). It is cheap and defensive, it suits NeurIPS E&D's interest in evaluation as an object of study and in rigorous negative results ([guidelines](https://neurips.cc/Conferences/2026/EvaluationsDatasetsReviewerGuidelines)), and no located emotion VLM paper covers option order. The second is an AU forward and reverse test for CCN 2027, with Feb 1 and Feb 4 deadlines for abstract and 8-page paper and Apr 1 for two-page abstracts ([CCN](https://www.ccneuro.org/)). The third is CFA plus prior-evidence decomposition for NeurIPS 2027, whose date is unannounced; the 2026 deadlines were May 4 and 6 ([future meetings](https://neurips.cc/Conferences/FutureMeetings)). ICML 2027's mid-to-late January dates are a third-party projection ([tracker](https://www.opencurious.com/ai-conference-deadlines/icml-2027)) and about 11 weeks away, too soon for a multi-domain paper with an unproved theorem and unvalidated renders. Gershman's lab has no face or emotion paper located ([publications](https://gershmanlab.com/pubs.html)), but Xiang et al.'s named-strategy fits offer a template for reporting a strategy difference rather than a score ([CogSci 2025](https://cicl.stanford.edu/publication/xiang2025how/)) [snippet].

## Conclusion

The new human evidence shifts where the study's scientific risk sits. The old worry was whether models hold a human-like confusion structure; the sharper worry is that part of that structure was never perceptual, because the leading pairs are one-way and rest on labels that faces do not transmit. Likewise, the supervisor's head-angle example turns out to be a poor test, while the better one, speed, cannot reach the model through the current pipeline. A cue can only be used if it is shown, offered and reliably labelled, and that delivery audit should precede every claim about mechanism.

The supervisor's strongest demand can be met only asymmetrically. Causal evidence inside the model is cheap and fully under the study's control. Causal evidence for humans is free only where someone has already randomised a cue, which on the data inspected so far means AUs on a minority of labels. The study should therefore state its human-referenced reach label by label, and treat every extension beyond it as a new human experiment rather than a stronger analysis. That restraint is also the novelty: no located study has yet held a VLM to randomised human cue effects at all.
