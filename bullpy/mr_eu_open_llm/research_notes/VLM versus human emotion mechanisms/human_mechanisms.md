# How humans identify emotions and complex mental states from faces and face video: an evidence-graded catalogue of testable mechanisms

How to read these notes. Compiled 2026-11-04 for the VLM versus human comparison on EU-Emotion silent face video and RMET. Every paper cited here was located; DOIs were matched by bibliographic query against Crossref, and abstracts came from OpenAlex or PubMed.

- Default verification level is "abstract read". Claims are limited to what the abstract states unless marked otherwise.
- [meta] means bibliographic record confirmed but no abstract retrieved, so only the title-level claim is used.
- [full text] means the full text was read (via a fetch tool that summarises pages, so treat exact numbers as worth rechecking).
- [secondary] means the detail comes from a non-primary summary.
- [snippet] means the source was seen only as a search-result summary.
- Grades: strong = replicated causal or psychophysical manipulation; moderate = real but limited to few labs, narrow stimuli or correlational data; contested = active conflicting evidence.
- Scope tags: BASIC = known only for six basic emotions; COMPLEX = direct evidence for complex or social states; BOTH.
- Human evidence type: STIM = intervention on the stimulus; OBS = intervention on or lesion of the observer; CORR = correlational.
- Every "Prediction for model" states the human baseline expected from the literature. Almost none of these baselines were measured on EU-Emotion, so each needs a human arm on the same manipulated clips before a model can be said to pass or fail (see Inferences under the last question).

## Which facial information drives human judgement?

### Takeaway
Human categorisation of basic expressions is driven by emotion-specific facial movements in specific regions (mouth for happy and disgust, eyes and brows for fear, sad, anger), read at task-dependent spatial scales and integrated over time. For the supervisor's question: head angle alone does not have a located demonstration of flipping happy to sad. It changes perceived dominance by mimicking a brow action, and it degrades recognition of eye-dependent emotions. The manipulations with direct evidence of shifting a category are playback speed (sad versus happy or anger), facial region composition, gaze on ambiguous blends, and body or situation context.

### Cited Findings

#### M1. Facial action configurations are sufficient to elicit category percepts (generative-model evidence)
- Synthetic faces built only from theoretically postulated muscle configurations were recognised far above chance with confusion patterns like posed photos; dynamic presentation raised accuracy and reduced confusions — [Wehrle et al. 2000, JPSP](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.78.1.105).
- Reverse correlation with a generative face grammar models the movements each observer expects for a category; culture changes those models: Westerners represent the six emotions with distinct movement sets, East Asians do not, and East Asians represent intensity with eye activity — [Jack et al. 2012, PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1200155109). Generator: [Yu, Garrod and Schyns 2012, Computers and Graphics](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cag.2011.12.002) [meta]. Method reviews: [Jack and Schyns 2015, Current Biology](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2015.05.052); [Jack and Schyns 2017, Annual Review of Psychology](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-010416-044242).
- A framework that turns AU hypotheses into predictive models compared them on human categorisations in Western and East Asian observers, tested the causal role of individual AUs, and provides a noise ceiling for what AUs versus individual differences can explain; it covers six emotions and four conversational signals — [Snoek et al. 2023, Science Advances](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abq8421). Note: the brief lists Snoek under context; this paper is an AU-model test, not a context study.
- Facial signals can multiplex: in 60 participants, models for six classic plus 19 complex emotions and for valence and arousal share subsets of components, so category and dimension are carried by shared latent signals (white Western participants and English terms only) — [Liu et al. 2022, Current Biology](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2021.10.035).
- Production is a different question. Meta-analytic co-occurrence of an emotion with its predicted full expression was .13 (.23 including partial expressions), and intensity correlation .30 — [Durán and Fernández-Dols 2021, Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0001015). Reliable AUs tend to be shared across emotions rather than specific — [Mehu et al. 2012, Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0026717). The Duchenne marker lacks specificity and depends on context — [Girard et al. 2020 preprint](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/397af); [Krumhuber and Manstead 2009, Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0017844).
- Evidence type: STIM (perception), CORR (production).
- Prediction for model: synthesise or edit a neutral EU-Emotion frame to add a single AU cluster (for example AU6+12 versus AU1+4+15). Humans shift towards the corresponding category, with AU-level effects estimable as in Snoek. A model passes if its label distribution shifts for the same AUs in the same direction with similar relative AU weights. It fails if it shifts only with identity or background artefacts of the edit.
- Grade: strong for perception of BASIC; moderate for COMPLEX (Liu 2022, Scherer 2018, see M21); contested as a claim that the AU reveals felt emotion.

#### M2. Region diagnosticity is emotion-specific; eyes are not universally dominant
- Bubbles reveals which image information supports a task — [Gosselin and Schyns 2001, Vision Research](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0042-6989(01)00097-9) [meta]. Applied to the six expressions to characterise transmitted and decoded information — [Smith et al. 2005, Psychological Science](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.0956-7976.2005.00801.x).
- Using 48-tile uncovering, expressions lie on a continuum from sadness and fear (eyes) to disgust and happiness (mouth), and diagnostic parts match AU locations — [Wegrzyn et al. 2017, PLOS ONE](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0177239).
- Across eight static and dynamic expressions the mouth was the most important and most informative cue, and observers underused the eyes relative to an ideal observer — [Blais et al. 2012, Neuropsychologia](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2012.08.010).
- Fixations follow diagnostic regions: [Eisenbarth and Alpers 2011, Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0022758); [Schurgin et al. 2014, Journal of Vision](https://doi.org/10.1167/14.13.14) (also goal-driven when seeking emotion in neutral faces); [Bombari et al. 2013, QJEP](https://doi.org/10.1080/17470218.2013.789065); [Beaudry et al. 2014, Cognition and Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1080/02699931.2013.833500), which cautions that the pattern is consistent mainly for happiness and sadness. Early gaze preferences appear even when a saccade could add no information — [Scheller et al. 2012, PLoS ONE](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0041792). Fixation and information use are not the same: dynamic faces draw fewer eye and mouth fixations with no systematic change in information used — [Blais et al. 2017, Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0000283).
- Natural occlusion experiments: masks reduce recognition and produce specific confusions (disgust read as anger; happy, sad, angry read as neutral) — [Carbon 2020, Frontiers in Psychology](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.566886); [Grundmann et al. 2021, PLOS ONE](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0249792); [Pazhoohi et al. 2021, PLOS ONE](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0257740). Masks and sunglasses cost about the same in one study — [Noyes et al. 2021, Royal Society Open Science](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.201169).
- Culture changes sampling: Eastern observers persistently fixate the eyes and thereby sample ambiguous information, producing fear and disgust confusions — [Jack et al. 2009, Current Biology](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2009.07.051). See also [Yuki et al. 2007, JESP](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jesp.2006.02.004) [meta].
- Evidence type: STIM.
- Prediction for model: for each clip, apply gaussian masks to eyes-and-brows, mouth, and both. Humans should lose happy and disgust with mouth masking, fear and sad with eye masking, and read masked negative faces as neutral. A model passes if it reproduces this label-by-region interaction and the confusion direction (disgust to anger). Report a Bubbles-style map on the model and correlate it with a human map.
- Grade: strong (BASIC). Not established for the EU-Emotion complex labels; see M22.

#### M3. Spatial frequency use is task-dependent and the fearful-eyes low-SF story is contested
- Different expressions transmit at different viewing distances, giving distal and proximal signals — [Smith and Schyns 2009, Psychological Science](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2009.02427.x). Happy identification depended on low SF and sad on high SF — [Kumar and Srinivasan 2011, Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0025453).
- The categorisation task changes which scale is used and perceived — [Schyns and Oliva 1999, Cognition](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0010-0277(98)00069-9). Spatial-frequency use changes with the comparison categories in the task — [Smith and Merlusca 2014, Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0035588).
- Amygdala responses to fearful expressions were greater for intact or low-SF faces, with subcortical activation specific to low SF — [Vuilleumier et al. 2003, Nature Neuroscience](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn1057). Statistical analysis found low SF a better source for categorising fear — [Mermillod et al. 2009, Connection Science](https://doi.org/10.1080/09540090802213974). Masked fearful eye whites alone drive amygdala — [Whalen et al. 2004, Science](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1103617).
- Contested by: similar amygdala response to low and high SF — [Morawetz et al. 2011, Brain Research](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brainres.2010.10.110); a rapid pulvino-amygdala route not modulated by SF or emotion — [McFadyen et al. 2017, Journal of Neuroscience](https://doi.org/10.1523/jneurosci.3525-16.2017); the review argument against a dominant subcortical route — [Pessoa and Adolphs 2010, Nature Reviews Neuroscience](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn2920); and methodological criticism of the low-SF subcortical inference — [De Cesarei and Codispoti 2013, Reviews in the Neurosciences](https://doi.org/10.1515/revneuro-2012-0053).
- Evidence type: STIM.
- Prediction for model: low-pass and high-pass each clip across a range of cutoffs in cycles per face. Humans should keep happy under low-pass and lose sad; high-pass should do the reverse. Changing the option set should change which band matters (Smith and Merlusca). A model that uses one fixed band regardless of foils, or tracks high-SF texture only, fails. Do not interpret any low-SF advantage as evidence of a subcortical analogue.
- Grade: moderate for behavioural SF tuning and task dependence (BASIC); contested for the low-road and low-SF amygdala account.

#### M4. Horizontal orientation band
- Emotion recognition largely depends on horizontal information defined relative to the face, modulated by factors such as mouth openness — [Huynh and Balas 2014, Attention, Perception, and Psychophysics](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13414-014-0669-4). N170 amplitude tracked horizontal information availability before its peak — [Duncan et al. 2018, Journal of Vision abstract](https://doi.org/10.1167/18.10.909).
- Evidence type: STIM.
- Prediction for model: orientation-filter frames to keep horizontal or vertical energy. Humans should do better with horizontal, with the benefit tied to the face frame (it should rotate with the face). CNN or ViT models with texture bias may not show this; a horizontal advantage defined in image rather than face coordinates fails.
- Grade: moderate (BASIC).

#### M5. Eye-opening physics: widening versus narrowing
- Fear widening increases visual field and eye movement speed, disgust narrowing does the opposite — [Susskind et al. 2008, Nature Neuroscience](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.2138). Fear eye widening helps observers too, via a greater physical signal from exposed iris and sclera rather than perceived emotion or attention — [Lee et al. 2013, Psychological Science](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797612464500).
- Directly for complex states: opposed eye widening and narrowing convey opposed clusters of complex mental states (sensitivity versus discrimination, for example awe versus suspicion); this dimension explained 61.7% of variance in perceived mental states, and held against competing lower-face information — [Lee and Anderson 2017, Psychological Science](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797616687364).
- Evidence type: STIM.
- Prediction for model: warp only eye aperture on EU-Emotion or RMET crops, leaving everything else. Humans should move towards interested, surprised, afraid when widened and towards unfriendly, sneaky, suspicious-type readings when narrowed, including with a conflicting lower face. A model passes if logits for these clusters change monotonically with aperture. This is a one-parameter intervention, cheap to run, and among the most direct for COMPLEX labels.
- Grade: moderate (COMPLEX and BASIC; mainly one lab).

#### M6. Gaze direction interacts with expression (shared signal hypothesis)
- Direct gaze facilitates approach emotions (anger, joy), averted gaze facilitates avoidance emotions (fear, sadness) — [Adams and Kleck 2003, Psychological Science](https://doi.org/10.1046/j.0956-7976.2003.psci_1479.x); [Adams and Kleck 2005, Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1037/1528-3542.5.1.3), including trait attribution to neutral faces and ratings of anger-fear blends. Amygdala link: [Adams et al. 2003, Science](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1082244). Replicated with dynamic synthetic faces, with appraisal theory as explanation — [Sander et al. 2007, European Journal of Cognitive Psychology](https://doi.org/10.1080/09541440600757426).
- Boundary conditions: the interaction appears only for mild, not high-intensity, expressions — [N'Diaye et al. 2009, Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0017845). Gaze affects expression only when the emotion is hard to discriminate — [Graham and LaBar 2007, Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1037/1528-3542.7.2.296). Amygdala effects depend on exposure duration — [Adams et al. 2012, SCAN](https://doi.org/10.1093/scan/nsr038) and on anxiety — [Ewbank et al. 2010, Frontiers in Human Neuroscience](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2010.00056).
- Direct contradiction: recognition of angry, fearful, happy and sad expressions was impaired by averted gaze, and earlier enhancement was described as stimulus- and task-bound — [Bindemann et al. 2008, Visual Cognition](https://doi.org/10.1080/13506280701269318).
- Evidence type: STIM.
- Prediction for model: gaze-redirect ambiguous or low-intensity clips only. Humans should show a stronger fear reading with averted gaze and a stronger anger reading with direct gaze, and no effect at high intensity. A model passes only if it shows the interaction scaled by intensity. A main effect of gaze across all clips does not count as a pass.
- Grade: contested (BASIC). No located test on complex labels except gaze as cue to thinking (M22).

#### M7. Head pose: action-unit imposter, not a category flip
- A downward head tilt increased perceived dominance through the illusory appearance of lowered, V-shaped brows, so pose imitates an action unit — [Witkower and Tracy 2019, Psychological Science](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797619838762) (the abstract specifies neutral faces). Upward versus downward head position also distinguishes prestige and dominance displays — [Witkower et al. 2020, JPSP](https://doi.org/10.1037/pspi0000181). Earlier: [Mignault and Chaudhuri 2003, Journal of Nonverbal Behavior](https://doi.org/10.1023/a:1023914509763) [meta]; [Hess et al. 2007, Motivation and Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11031-007-9057-x) [meta].
- Yaw: profile views reduce perceived intensity while the gaze distribution stays qualitatively viewpoint-independent, and authors argue for viewpoint-invariant categorical expression processing — [Guo and Shaw 2015, Acta Psychologica](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actpsy.2014.12.001). Fear, anger and sadness were better recognised frontally than in profile, with no difference for other emotions, read as a cost only for eye-dependent emotions — [Surcinelli et al. 2022, Psychological Reports](https://doi.org/10.1177/00332941211018403).
- Head as signal for complex states: perceived embarrassed smiles had greater forward head pitch than perceived polite or amused smiles — [Ambadar et al. 2009, Journal of Nonverbal Behavior](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10919-008-0059-5). Negative correlation between lip corner displacement and head pitch was read as embarrassment — [Cohn et al. 2004, IEEE FG](https://doi.org/10.1109/afgr.2004.1301520). Pride includes the head tilted slightly back — [Tracy and Robins 2004, Psychological Science](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.0956-7976.2004.01503008.x). Observers decode emotion from silent, face-occluded head movement during speech and song — [Livingstone and Palmer 2016, Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0000106).
- Rigid versus nonrigid motion: intrinsic facial movement is necessary for a dynamic expression adaptation effect; rigid head motion only modulates it when intrinsic motion is present — [de la Rosa et al. 2013, Journal of Vision](https://doi.org/10.1167/13.1.23). Rigid head, eye, brow and mouth motion together suffice for conversational expressions — [Cunningham et al. 2005, ACM TAP](https://doi.org/10.1145/1077399.1077404).
- Evidence type: STIM.
- Prediction for model: (a) Rotate pitch on neutral and low-intensity clips with a 3D head model and re-render. Humans should report more dominant, unfriendly or angry readings when tilted down, without a change to happy or sad categories. Pass requires the shift to be mediated by brow appearance: masking the brows should remove it. (b) Yaw rotation should cost fear, anger and sad more than happy. (c) Stabilise heads (remove rigid motion by face alignment) on Ashamed, Proud and Embarrassed-like clips: humans should lose some discrimination, since pitch is itself part of those displays. (d) The supervisor's direct test, "does pose flip happy to sad": the literature predicts no flip in humans for a clear smile. A model that flips is showing non-human dependence on pose.
- Grade: moderate (dominance imposter from one lab with a replicated line; yaw from small studies; COMPLEX evidence only for pride and embarrassment).

#### M8. Dynamic advantage is conditional on signal quality
- Motion improves identification of subtle expressions through perception of change — [Ambadar et al. 2005, Psychological Science](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.0956-7976.2005.01548.x). The advantage comes from the motion signal, and falls with intensity — [Bould and Morris 2008, British Journal of Psychology](https://doi.org/10.1348/000712607x206702). Disrupting speed or rhythm reduces recognition — [Bould et al. 2008, Cognition and Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1080/02699930801921156). The temporal integration window for conversational expressions is at least 100 ms and the benefit requires information available only over time — [Cunningham and Wallraven 2009, Journal of Vision](https://doi.org/10.1167/9.13.7).
- Reviews: [Krumhuber et al. 2013, Emotion Review](https://doi.org/10.1177/1754073912451349); benefit appears most under poor viewing conditions, impaired observers, or degraded and subtle expressions — [Krumhuber et al. 2023, Nature Reviews Psychology](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44159-023-00172-1) [snippet of abstract].
- Ceiling explanation: at full signal, static and dynamic were equal except for happiness and surprise; with reduced signal dynamic was always better, peaking near 20% signal — [Richoz et al. 2024, Journal of Vision](https://doi.org/10.1167/jov.24.1.7) (n = 70, basic only).
- Counterexample for complex-relevant states: engagement labels of 10 s clips were predicted by averaging frame labels (r = .85), implying static frames carry most information for that judgement — [Whitehill et al. 2014, IEEE TAC](https://doi.org/10.1109/taffc.2014.2316163).
- Evidence type: STIM.
- Prediction for model: compare the full clip, a single apex frame, a frame bag (shuffled), and an order-preserved clip, crossed with phase-noise degradation at 100%, 50%, 20%. Humans should show a dynamic advantage that grows with degradation and for low-intensity labels. A VLM that subsamples frames should show no degradation-by-dynamics interaction. Interested and Bored may be the labels where frames suffice (Whitehill), so an advantage there would be informative, not a default.
- Grade: strong (BASIC, subtle expressions); moderate (COMPLEX: Cunningham and Wallraven).

#### M9. Temporal order: early coarse, late specific
- Early movements support fewer categories (approach and avoidance type), later movements support the six classic categories, suggesting four rather than six — [Jack et al. 2014, Current Biology](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2013.11.064). Early shared signals: surprise and fear (dropped jaw, raised eyelids), anger and disgust (wrinkled nose, funnelled lips); raised brows and sneered lip disambiguate later; n = 60 Western adults; happy and sad recognised early — [British Psychological Society Research Digest summary](https://www.bps.org.uk/research-digest/study-dynamic-facial-expressions-suggests-there-are-four-basic-emotions-not-six) [secondary; full text blocked].
- Four latent patterns across cultures — [Jack et al. 2016, JEP General](https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0000162). Space-by-time decomposition — [Delis et al. 2016, Journal of Vision](https://doi.org/10.1167/16.8.14). Across two cultures (n = 120), intensifier movements peak earlier or later than category classifiers and are more similar across emotions, implying a broad-plus-specific structure, with cultural differences for low-threat emotions — [Chen et al. 2024, Current Biology](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2023.12.001).
- Time reversal is a usable control: reversed timelines hold low-level motion energy constant — [Reinl and Bartels 2015, Frontiers in Psychology](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.01107).
- Evidence type: STIM (on synthetic faces).
- Prediction for model: truncate clips to the first third of the rise. Humans should confuse fear with surprise and anger with disgust early and resolve them late. Reverse clips: any order-sensitive observer should change, whereas a frame-bag model cannot, by construction. A model that shows identical logits for forward and reversed input has no temporal-order code.
- Grade: moderate (BASIC; synthetic faces). The four-category reading sits in tension with basic-emotion reviews reporting upwards of 20 emotions signalled in multimodal, dynamic patterns — [Keltner et al. 2019, Journal of Nonverbal Behavior](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10919-019-00293-3).

#### M10. Kinematics: speed shifts category, including sad versus happy
- Speeding up expressions promotes anger and happiness judgements, slowing them promotes sad judgements, and this is dissociable from spatial cues — [Sowden et al. 2021, Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0000835). Naturalness depends on velocity by emotion — [Sato and Yoshikawa 2004, Cognition and Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1080/02699930341000176).
- Genuine versus deliberate: dynamic components matter for smile genuineness — [Krumhuber and Kappas 2005, Journal of Nonverbal Behavior](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10919-004-0887-x) [meta]. Deliberate smiles have greater onset and offset speed, amplitude and offset duration — [Schmidt et al. 2006, Journal of Nonverbal Behavior](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10919-005-0003-x). Perceived amused, polite and embarrassed smiles differ in amplitude, onset and offset velocity, duration — [Ambadar et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10919-008-0059-5). Reward, affiliation and dominance smiles have distinct dynamic forms and social messages — [Rychlowska et al. 2017, Psychological Science](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797617706082).
- Evidence type: STIM.
- Prediction for model: resample playback at 0.5x, 1x, 2x on the same frames. Humans should move towards sad at slow speed and towards happy or angry at fast speed. This is the cleanest available happy-to-sad-direction intervention, needs no generative editing, and changes no pixel content. Pass requires a same-direction shift. A model that sees only sampled frames at a fixed rate receives altered temporal sampling, so log which frames were sent. If frame sets are identical across speeds, the test is uninformative and the model is structurally blind to speed.
- Grade: strong to moderate (speed-category effect from one lab with converging smile work; BASIC plus smile subtypes, which are COMPLEX-relevant).

#### M11. Non-AU visible signals: colour, tears, pupil
- Facial colour changes transmit emotion even without muscle activation and independently of AUs — [Benitez-Quiroz et al. 2018, PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1716084115). Blushing and paling: [Thorstenson et al. 2021, Motivation and Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11031-021-09910-5) [meta].
- Tears: digital tear removal made faces look less sad and often of uncertain valence (awe, concern, puzzlement, reported as anecdotal) — [Provine et al. 2009, Evolutionary Psychology](https://doi.org/10.1177/147470490900700107); adding tears speeds sadness recognition and raises perceived need for support — [Balsters et al. 2013, Evolutionary Psychology](https://doi.org/10.1177/147470491301100114).
- Pupil: observed pupil size is mirrored and engages sadness processing — [Harrison et al. 2006, SCAN](https://doi.org/10.1093/scan/nsl006). Review of autonomic signals — [Kret 2015, Frontiers in Psychology](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00711).
- Evidence type: STIM.
- Prediction for model: convert clips to greyscale and shift facial hue along the empirically derived emotion direction. Humans should lose little for high-AU labels and shift mildly for low-AU labels. RMET is greyscale already, so colour cannot explain RMET performance in either system. A model much more colour-dependent than humans reveals a non-human cue.
- Grade: moderate (BASIC; one main group for colour).

#### M12. Neutral-face structure is read as emotion (overgeneralisation) and confounds actor with label
- Warping neutral faces toward emotion-resembling structure, without any wrinkles, drives impressions — [Adams et al. 2012, Cognition and Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1080/02699931.2012.666502). Neutral face structure resembling emotions predicts evaluation — [Said et al. 2009, Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0016784) [meta]; [Zebrowitz et al. 2010, JPSP](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0017990). Trustworthiness impressions share a perceptual basis with expressions — [Oosterhof and Todorov 2009, Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0014520); space of evaluation — [Oosterhof and Todorov 2008, PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0805664105). Review: [Todorov et al. 2015, Annual Review of Psychology](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-113011-143831).
- Dynamic expressions of social traits can camouflage or override morphology; they are distinct signals, composed of movements from different emotions, and not simple overgeneralisation — [Gill et al. 2014, Psychological Science](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797614522274).
- Trait space is shaped by conceptual beliefs — [Stolier et al. 2020, Nature Human Behaviour](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-019-0800-6).
- Evidence type: STIM.
- Prediction for model: compute model output on each actor's neutral clip and test whether it predicts that actor's error pattern on other labels. Humans should show a small actor effect for Kind and Unfriendly. A model with a large actor main effect is relying on morphology or identity. This matters particularly because Kind and Unfriendly are trait-like labels.
- Grade: strong for trait impressions; moderate for influence on emotion labelling. Direct evidence for these labels on video is absent.

### Inferences
- The strongest model tests here are those that need no generative editing: masking, filtering, reversing, truncating, resampling. Pose and gaze interventions require rendering, which risks artefacts humans do not see, so any model effect should be checked against a sham edit (render with zero change).
- Three distinct "happy to sad" levers exist with human support: speed (M10), mouth composition (M2, M13) and context (M24). Head angle is not among them on available evidence.
- Most of the catalogue is BASIC. Extrapolating to Bored or Worried is a hypothesis, not a finding.

### Gaps
- No located study intervenes on head pose and measures a happy-to-sad or complex-label change.
- Mignault and Chaudhuri 2003 and Hess et al. 2007 are the obvious sources for pose-to-emotion mapping, but neither abstract was retrievable; I recall bowed head linked to submission and inferiority emotions, which is unverified.
- Jack 2014 main text checked 2026-11-07 (https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/88928/): the early and late AU order above holds. Supplement unread.

## Holistic versus featural processing: what is established for expression as opposed to identity?

### Takeaway
Expression shows a real composite effect and some inversion sensitivity, but inversion costs are smaller and more emotion-specific than for identity, and local features can carry recognition. Treat expression as mixed: holistic interference from misaligned parts plus strong featural diagnosticity. Nothing located establishes this for complex states.

### Cited Findings

#### M13. Composite expression effect
- Aligning top and bottom halves of different expressions slows identification of a half; misalignment relieves this, inversion disrupts it, it requires same-identity halves, and it operates independently of the identity composite effect — [Calder et al. 2000, JEP: HPP](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-1523.26.2.527).
- Holistic and analytic routes coexist: interference from an incongruent half appeared at 20 ms exposure, and spatial alignment hurt only incongruent expressions — [Tanaka et al. 2012, Cognition and Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1080/02699931.2011.630933). Composite and inversion effects occur in children and adults — [Durand et al. 2007, Journal of Experimental Child Psychology](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jecp.2006.12.001).
- Chimeric faces indicate features can suffice even when the global configuration is ambiguous, against a pure holistic account — [Fiorentini and Viviani 2009, Visual Cognition](https://doi.org/10.1080/13506280701821019). Holistic perception is tied to low SF (identity paradigm) — [Goffaux and Rossion 2006, JEP: HPP](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-1523.32.4.1023).
- Measurement caution: the composite effect is deceptively hard to measure and interpret — [Murphy et al. 2017, Psychonomic Bulletin and Review](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-016-1131-5) (identity focus).
- Ponari: neutral half-faces interfere differentially by emotion (lower half for happy and disgust, upper half for anger, both for fear and sad) — [Ponari et al. 2012, Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0028588). Those authors read this as mimicry, but it can also be read as region diagnosticity, and that alternative is my inference.
- Evidence type: STIM.
- Prediction for model: compose frames with upper half from one label and lower half from another, aligned versus offset by half a face width, on same actor. Humans should be slower or less accurate naming the attended half when aligned. VLMs have no reaction time, so instruct "report the top half only" and measure contamination of the output distribution by the bottom half. A pass needs contamination that falls when misaligned and when inverted. Fixed-patch tokenisers may retain alignment dependence for trivial reasons, so include a patch-shuffled control.
- Grade: moderate (BASIC).

#### M14. Inversion and Thatcherisation
- Inversion reduced accuracy for sad, fear, anger and disgust, and sad was read as neutral, while happy stayed almost perfect; classic confusions (surprise-fear, disgust-anger) persisted, supporting a mix of configural and componential processing — [McKelvie 1995, British Journal of Social Psychology](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2044-8309.1995.tb01067.x). Only some expressions change processing — [Prkachin 2003, British Journal of Psychology](https://doi.org/10.1348/000712603762842093). Effects depend on exposure time and emotion — [Derntl et al. 2009, Perception](https://doi.org/10.1068/p6448). Featural and configural dependence varies by emotion — [Bombari et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1080/17470218.2013.789065).
- Inversion causes only a small reduction, local feature inversion in upright faces causes a much larger one, and inverting a Thatcherised face improves recognition — [Psalta and Andrews 2014, Perception](https://doi.org/10.1068/p7755). The STS shows orientation-dependent release from adaptation for Thatcherised faces — [Psalta et al. 2014, Psychological Science](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797613501521). Generic Thatcher configural disruption — [Lewis and Johnston 1997, Perception](https://doi.org/10.1068/p260225).
- Evidence type: STIM.
- Prediction for model: invert whole clips; separately Thatcherise eye and mouth regions. Humans: small inversion cost on sad, fear, anger and disgust, none on happy, with inverted sad read as neutral; Thatcherisation costs more upright than inverted. A model with an equal cost across labels, or with no interaction of feature inversion by orientation, processes faces differently. Inversion is the simplest cheap holistic screen, and a large model drop would be informative because CNNs are known to be orientation-sensitive (inference, not a located finding here).
- Grade: strong that inversion cost is small and emotion-specific relative to identity (BASIC); moderate for Thatcher.

### Inferences
- "Holistic" in expression research largely means interference from aligned parts. It should not be equated with a whole-image model.
- Because a composite paradigm depends on stimuli with unambiguous half-face information, it may not work for Bored or Worried. Run it first on happy, sad, angry, afraid, disgusted subsets.

### Gaps
- No located composite, inversion or Thatcher study for complex mental states, including the RMET item set or EU-Emotion.
- I found no direct head-to-head effect size comparing identity and expression inversion within one sample in the located abstracts.

## Causal human evidence: lesions, TMS, prosopagnosia, mimicry blockade, adaptation, categorical perception

### Takeaway
Observer-side causal evidence is strongest for three claims: amygdala-dependent gaze to the eyes supports fear recognition; static and dynamic face routes are dissociable; and expression has adaptable, identity-partly-independent high-level codes. Facial mimicry is the most contested: blockade effects exist but Moebius evidence shows it is not necessary. Morph categorical perception is well established behaviourally, with the role of language disputed.

### Cited Findings

#### M15. Amygdala, gaze and the eyes (patient SM and others)
- SM showed impaired fear recognition and impaired recognition of multiple emotions in one face, with spared identity — [Adolphs et al. 1994, Nature](https://doi.org/10.1038/372669a0). The deficit arises from failure to use eye information, and instruction to look at the eyes restored fear recognition to normal — [Adolphs et al. 2005, Nature](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature03086). Her failure is at the first saccade and reflects bottom-up attraction to the eyes — [Kennedy and Adolphs 2010, Neuropsychologia](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2010.06.025). Patient MW lacks reflexive saccades to diagnostic features at brief presentation, but performs normally with longer viewing — [Gamer et al. 2013, Current Biology](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2013.09.008). Amygdala activity predicts gaze to fearful eyes — [Gamer and Büchel 2009, Journal of Neuroscience](https://doi.org/10.1523/jneurosci.1883-09.2009).
- Non-uniform lesion outcomes: basolateral damage gave fear hypervigilance, including longer attention to fearful eyes — [Terburg et al. 2012, Translational Psychiatry](https://doi.org/10.1038/tp.2012.46). A patient with no functional amygdala engaged premotor and parietal regions to fearful faces, read as compensation — [Becker et al. 2012, Biological Psychiatry](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2011.11.024).
- Complex states: amygdala patients were more impaired on social than basic emotions, including from the eye region alone — [Adolphs et al. 2002, Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience](https://doi.org/10.1162/089892902760807258). Two patients with adult-onset damage were impaired on ToM tasks including Eyes — [Stone et al. 2003, Neuropsychologia](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0028-3932(02)00151-3).
- Amygdala neurons encode perceived or judged emotion rather than image array — [Wang et al. 2014, PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1323342111), plus degree of emotion and categorical ambiguity — [Wang et al. 2017, Nature Communications](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms14821).
- Evidence type: OBS (very small n), STIM (instruction).
- Prediction for model: SM's result separates information availability from information use. Measure model attribution on fearful clips. If attribution is off the eyes, add a pointer: crop to eyes, or prompt "look at the eyes". The human precedent is that fear recovers with no other change. A model where the eye crop improves fear and only fear, relative to whole face, shows the same bottleneck. A model that is unchanged is not eye-limited. In-model version: ablate eye-region patch tokens and test selective fear loss.
- Grade: strong (BASIC, for fear); moderate (COMPLEX: small n).

#### M16. Dissociable static and dynamic cortical routes
- Right OFA TMS impairs face-part discrimination at 60 and 100 ms — [Pitcher et al. 2007, Current Biology](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2007.07.063). Expression discrimination is impaired by TMS to rOFA at 60-100 ms and right somatosensory cortex at 100-170 ms — [Pitcher et al. 2008, Journal of Neuroscience](https://doi.org/10.1523/jneurosci.1450-08.2008).
- Double dissociation in rpSTS response: rOFA disruption reduces the static face response, rpSTS disruption reduces the dynamic — [Pitcher et al. 2014, Current Biology](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2014.07.060). Behavioural follow-up: [Sliwinska et al. 2020, Cortex](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cortex.2020.03.033).
- Lesion evidence: double dissociation of static and dynamic face perception in 108 focal-lesion patients, read as causal evidence for a third pathway via pSTS — [Prabhakar et al. 2025, Nature Communications](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-61395-9). Early patient evidence that moving and static expression are separately computed — [Humphreys et al. 1993, Neuropsychologia](https://doi.org/10.1016/0028-3932(93)90045-2). Prosopagnosic PS decodes dynamic expressions with reverse correlation but not many static ones — [Richoz et al. 2015, Cortex](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cortex.2014.11.015).
- Information content: in MEG (N = 10), a social pathway from occipital cortex to superior temporal gyrus selectively represents movements needed for categorisation, filtering identity early — [Yan et al. 2025, PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2423560122). Framework: [Pitcher and Ungerleider 2021, TiCS](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2020.11.006); [Duchaine and Yovel 2015, Annual Review of Vision Science](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-vision-082114-035518); [Bernstein and Yovel 2015, Neuroscience and Biobehavioral Reviews](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2015.06.010).
- Evidence type: OBS.
- Prediction for model: humans have partly separate form and motion routes. Lesion analogue: zero out temporal position embeddings, or replace video with per-frame average features; compare a form-only and motion-only input (optical flow or point-light rendering of landmarks). Humans still read expression from motion alone (Humphreys; point-light work, see Keating in M28). A model passes if motion-only input carries label information without any texture. Most VLMs likely fail, making this a discriminating test.
- Grade: strong.

#### M17. RMET-relevant lesion and TMS findings
- Lesion mapping in penetrating TBI linked RMET loss to left inferior frontal gyrus, read as semantic working memory for reasoning about others — [Dal Monte et al. 2014, Cortex](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cortex.2014.05.002). Double dissociation of emotional empathy (IFG) from cognitive empathy (ventromedial) — [Shamay-Tsoory et al. 2009, Brain](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awn279). rTMS over right DLPFC accelerated cognitive but not affective ToM — [Kalbe et al. 2010, Cortex](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cortex.2009.07.010). Oxytocin improved RMET, especially on hard items — [Domes et al. 2007, Biological Psychiatry](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2006.07.015).
- Evidence type: OBS.
- Prediction for model: if RMET success depends on semantic working memory (left IFG), then RMET model accuracy should degrade under label substitution (rare synonyms, definitions) much more than under visual noise of comparable effect. See M23.
- Grade: moderate (COMPLEX; single lesion study). Oxytocin is contested; see Gaps.

#### M18. Prosopagnosia and identity versus expression
- Normal expression recognition with severe identity loss — [Duchaine et al. 2003, Perception](https://doi.org/10.1068/p5067); [Humphreys et al. 2007, Experimental Brain Research](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00221-006-0621-5) (congenital spared even for subtle expressions, acquired impaired).
- Against full separation: 17 developmental prosopagnosics were impaired in expression classification, particularly apperceptive cases, suggesting an early shared locus — [Biotti and Cook 2016, Cortex](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cortex.2016.04.008). Congenital cases with normal expression and social-expression recognition show weaker holistic processing for both, suggesting compensation — [Palermo et al. 2011, Neuropsychologia](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2011.02.021). See also [Burns et al. 2017, Neuropsychologia](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2017.06.020) [meta].
- PS uses the mouth even when eyes are diagnostic, and her deficit persists even when forced to look at the eyes — [Fiset et al. 2017, SCAN](https://doi.org/10.1093/scan/nsx068). This contrasts sharply with SM (M15): fixation restored SM but not PS.
- Common dimensions: identity and expression aftereffects correlate and predict recognition of both — [Rhodes et al. 2015, Cognition](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2015.05.012). Critique of two-pathway dichotomy — [Calder and Young 2005, Nature Reviews Neuroscience](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn1724).
- Evidence type: OBS, CORR.
- Prediction for model: across the 17 actors, test whether label decoding is independent of identity decoding in the model's representation: identity swap, or leave-one-actor-out readout. Humans would show partial invariance (Fox and Barton, M20). Expect label representations to be separable from identity but not orthogonal. Report the SM versus PS distinction: if forcing eye input does not help, the model resembles a representational rather than an attentional failure.
- Grade: contested (mostly BASIC; Palermo includes social expressions).

#### M19. Facial mimicry and sensorimotor simulation
- For: chewing and biting manipulations selectively affect happiness — [Oberman et al. 2007, Social Neuroscience](https://doi.org/10.1080/17470910701391943). Blocking makes true and false smiles look the same — [Rychlowska et al. 2014, PLoS ONE](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0090876). Botox impairs and restricting gel improves emotion perception — [Neal and Chartrand 2011, SPPS](https://doi.org/10.1177/1948550611406138). Facemask gel impairs visual discrimination of expressions — [Wood et al. 2016, Psychonomic Bulletin and Review](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-015-0974-5). Concurrent motor load (vowel production) reduces precision of expression categorisation, not gender — [Ipser and Cook 2016, JEP: HPP](https://doi.org/10.1037/xhp0000177). Botox slows slightly emotional face categorisation — [Baumeister et al. 2016, Toxicon](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.toxicon.2016.04.044). Lower-face blocking hinders happy recognition from faces and bodies — [Borgomaneri et al. 2020, PLoS ONE](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0229364). Somatosensory TMS: M16. Deliberate mimicry skill predicts recognition, with preregistered replication (n = 50) — [Amihai et al. 2026, Affective Science](https://doi.org/10.1007/s42761-026-00366-9). Theory: [Wood et al. 2016, TiCS](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2015.12.010); [Niedenthal et al. 2010, BBS](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0140525x10000865). Botox and amygdala: [Kim et al. 2014, Biology of Mood and Anxiety Disorders](https://doi.org/10.1186/2045-5380-4-11).
- Against: 37 adults with Moebius syndrome did not differ from controls and accuracy was unrelated to expressive ability — [Rives Bogart and Matsumoto 2010, Social Neuroscience](https://doi.org/10.1080/17470910903395692). A person with congenital absence of facial motor representations reached normotypical efficient recognition — [Vannuscorps et al. 2020, eLife](https://doi.org/10.7554/elife.54687). Meta-analysis of 162 effects from 28 studies found a weak relation of mimicry to empathy, but not to recognition — [Holland et al. 2021, Cognition and Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1080/02699931.2020.1815655). Mimicry did not mediate smile judgements — [Korb et al. 2014, PLoS ONE](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0099194). Pen-in-mouth produced no recognition effect for sounds, frequentist and Bayesian — [Wołoszyn et al. 2026, Scientific Reports](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-43390-2) (sounds, not faces). Moebius may also involve visual differences — [Bate et al. 2013, PLoS ONE](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0062656), so its null is not simple, and in that study five of six showed expression impairments.
- Facial feedback replication status, which concerns experience, not recognition: 17 direct replications found 0.03 units against the original 0.82 — [Wagenmakers et al. 2016, Perspectives on Psychological Science](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691616674458). Camera presence was proposed as a moderator — [Noah et al. 2018, JPSP](https://doi.org/10.1037/pspa0000121). Meta-analysis found small, heterogeneous effects — [Coles et al. 2019, Psychological Bulletin](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000194). Preregistered multi-lab adversarial collaboration (n = 3,878, 19 countries) supported mimicry and voluntary facial action but was less conclusive for pen-in-mouth — [Coles et al. 2022, Nature Human Behaviour](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-022-01458-9).
- Evidence type: OBS.
- Prediction for model: no direct intervention exists because models have no motor system, which is itself the test. If simulation were necessary for humans, a model should not match humans on subtle smiles or genuine versus false smiles. Prediction under "not necessary": a visual model can match, so humans under mimicry blockade would be expected to look more like the model than unblocked humans do. Run a human blockade arm (mouthguard) on subtle clips and compare the human-model RDM fit with and without blockade. A fit increase under blockade would imply that the model lacks a contribution that sensorimotor state supplies.
- Grade: contested (BASIC plus smile authenticity). Moebius evidence rules out necessity, and modulatory roles remain open.

#### M20. Adaptation aftereffects
- Adaptation affects natural categorical face judgements — [Webster et al. 2004, Nature](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature02420) (the abstract names expression among the judged attributes but does not report the expression result separately); expression adaptation — [Hsu and Young 2004, Visual Cognition](https://doi.org/10.1080/13506280444000030). Adapting to a face biases morph perception away from the adapted expression; a different image of the same person works equally well, different identities give smaller but significant effects ("visual semantic"), and non-face, auditory or verbal emotion stimuli do not adapt — [Fox and Barton 2007, Brain Research](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brainres.2006.09.104). Transfer across identity is confirmed across five expressions — [Campbell and Burke 2009, Vision Research](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.visres.2009.03.009); see also [Ellamil et al. 2008, CABN](https://doi.org/10.3758/cabn.8.3.273) [meta].
- Level of coding: aftereffects rely on face configuration, not local elements — [Butler et al. 2008, Brain Research](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brainres.2007.10.101); yet adapting to curves shifts expression judgements, so a local component exists — [Xu et al. 2008, Journal of Neuroscience](https://doi.org/10.1523/jneurosci.0182-08.2008). These two conflict in part.
- Structure: anti-expressions yield aftereffects towards the matching expression, implying prototype-referenced coding — [Skinner and Benton 2010, Psychological Science](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797610380702); selectivity — [Juricevic and Webster 2012, Frontiers in Psychology](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2012.00004). Asymmetry: negative adapters yield happy, happy yields sad — [Rutherford et al. 2008, JEP: HPP](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-1523.34.1.27). Review: [Minemoto and Ueda 2022, Frontiers in Psychology](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.988497).
- This is a direct happy-sad intervention: prolonged exposure to a sad face pushes an ambiguous face towards happy, without any change to the test image.
- Evidence type: STIM.
- Prediction for model: present a repeated adapter image (or several frames) before a morph test inside one message. Humans shift away from the adapter, with a smaller shift across identities. Stateless feed-forward models should show no shift; a multi-image-context model might show priming towards, not away from, the adapter. An assimilative rather than repulsive shift would be a clear qualitative fail and shows contextual priming rather than adaptation.
- Grade: strong (BASIC). Not tested for complex labels.

#### M20b. Categorical perception of morphs
- Across-boundary pairs are better discriminated than within-category pairs — [Etcoff and Magee 1992, Cognition](https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-0277(92)90002-y); photographic morphs, and not a range artefact or memory phenomenon — [Calder et al. 1996, Visual Cognition](https://doi.org/10.1080/713756735). Megamix: sharp boundaries yet sensitivity to the mix — [Young et al. 1997, Cognition](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0010-0277(97)00003-6).
- Neural: pSTS represents continuous change, amygdala category change — [Harris et al. 2012, PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1212207110). pSTS holds distributed expression codes — [Said et al. 2010, Journal of Vision](https://doi.org/10.1167/10.5.11). AU-specific coding in pSTS — [Srinivasan et al. 2016, Journal of Neuroscience](https://doi.org/10.1523/jneurosci.1704-15.2016). Experience shifts boundaries: abused children show altered anger categorisation — [Pollak and Kistler 2002, PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.142165999).
- Language role disputed: verbal interference removes CP for expressions — [Roberson and Davidoff 2000, Memory and Cognition](https://doi.org/10.3758/bf03209345) [meta]. Yucatec Maya speakers lacking a disgust-anger lexical split show the same CP — [Sauter et al. 2011, Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0025336). CP for chimpanzee faces needed learned labels — [Fugate et al. 2010, Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0019017). Review: [Fugate 2013, Emotion Review](https://doi.org/10.1177/1754073912451350).
- Evidence type: STIM.
- Prediction for model: build happy-sad, fear-anger, and fear-surprise morph continua in 10% steps on same-actor apex frames. Humans give a sigmoid identification function and a discrimination peak at the boundary. Fit the slope of model label probability. Model discrimination proxy: same-different on pairs, or embedding distance. Pass requires a discrimination peak located at the model's own boundary. Linear probability ramps with linear embedding distance fail. Morphing is the direct way to test the happy-to-sad boundary and estimate where each system places it.
- Grade: strong (BASIC); contested for language dependence; absent for complex states.

### Inferences
- A key contrast for the study: SM (attention failure, fixable by pointing at eyes) versus PS (representation failure, not fixable). A model analogue of "forced fixation" (crop or token mask) distinguishes which failure a model has.
- Mimicry cannot be intervened on in a model, so it is better treated as a question about what a purely visual system can reach. Do not build it into the claim that models are "non-embodied and therefore different" without the blockade comparison.

### Gaps
- Replication of Domes 2007: I recall large later null or mixed results and methodological critiques, but did not locate and verify a specific paper in this pass.
- No preregistered, large-sample mimicry blockade test on face recognition was located; the 2026 preregistered null concerns sounds. Facial-feedback replications concern subjective experience, not perception of others.
- No adaptation or morph CP study on complex mental states located.

## Complex mental states: which cues exist, is the eye region sufficient, and how much is perception versus concepts?

### Takeaway
Direct evidence for cues to Bored, Worried, Interested or Disappointed is thin, comes mainly from learning contexts and FACS description, and rarely uses causal stimulus manipulation. EU-Emotion's own validation shows the labels vary widely in face-only recoverability: Kind and Unfriendly about 9% chance-corrected from faces against 91% and 68% from body scenes. This suggests some targets are not face-read at all. Eye-region sufficiency rests on small early studies and on the RMET, whose validity is doubted and which loads on vocabulary and emotion perception. Concept knowledge demonstrably constrains discrete category perception, more strongly as labels get finer.

### Cited Findings

#### M21. Candidate cues for complex states (label by label)
- Set-level facts: EU-Emotion has 418 clips: 249 face, 82 body, 87 contextual social scenes; 2-52 s; 20 states plus neutral; 19 actors aged 10-70; 17 face actors each portraying 10 emotions plus neutral, so actor and label are only partly crossed. Validation: 1,231 typically developed adults, 14 surveys, UK, Sweden, Israel. Chance-corrected 6AFC mean face 63% (SD 16), body 77%, social scene 72%; basic emotions at high intensity 78%, low intensity 63%. Per-label examples, face: joking 90, surprised 79, jealous 14 (13 elsewhere in paper), kind 9, unfriendly 9. Body: frustrated 98, disappointed 97, kind 91, unfriendly 68, jealous 3. Social: frustrated 96, afraid 94, unfriendly 62, kind 61, jealous 44 — [O'Reilly et al. 2016, Behavior Research Methods](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-015-0601-4) [full text]. No AU or eye-tracking data reported there.
- Confusion, concentration and worry: asked to describe naturally occurring expressions, students often report confusion, think-concentrate and worry, all outside standard taxonomies; confusion is characterised by movements around the eyes — [Rozin and Cohen 2003, Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1037/1528-3542.3.1.68). This is the main located source on Worried, and it is naming data.
- Boredom, frustration, confusion during learning: FACS rules were found with kappa .76-.84 — [Craig et al. 2008, Cognition and Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1080/02699930701516759). AU4 correlated with frustration, AU14 with frustration and learning — [Grafsgaard et al. 2013, ACII](https://doi.org/10.1109/acii.2013.33). Affect dynamics: [D'Mello and Graesser 2012, Learning and Instruction](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2011.10.001) [meta]. Production and spontaneous data, not perception.
- Engagement, relevant to Interested and Bored: observers reliably separate low and high engagement (kappa .96) and frame-level labels predict clip labels — [Whitehill et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1109/taffc.2014.2316163).
- Interest: distinct expressive display and core relational theme — [Campos et al. 2013, Cognition and Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1080/02699931.2012.683852). FACS frequency and duration differ among interest, pride, pleasure, joy — [Mortillaro et al. 2011, SPPS](https://doi.org/10.1177/1948550610389080). [Reeve 1993, Motivation and Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1007/bf00992325) [meta]; conceptual: [Silvia 2008, Current Directions](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8721.2008.00548.x).
- Pride: small smile, head back, expanded posture, arms raised or hands on hips, so largely bodily — [Tracy and Robins 2004](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.0956-7976.2004.01503008.x). Embarrassment: distinct display, temporal pattern — [Keltner 1995, JPSP](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.68.3.441). 
- Conversational and cognitive expressions: 55 expressions of 19 people recognised well from visual information alone — [Kaulard et al. 2012, PLoS ONE](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0032321). Nine conversational expressions usually rely on one facial region, each a different one — [Cunningham et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1145/1077399.1077404); some need one region, others interaction — [Nusseck et al. 2008, Journal of Vision](https://doi.org/10.1167/8.8.1).
- Thinking and gaze: gaze aversion manages cognitive load — [Doherty-Sneddon and Phelps 2005, Memory and Cognition](https://doi.org/10.3758/bf03195338). Thinking gaze direction varies by culture (Canadians and Trinidadians up, Japanese down) — [McCarthy et al. 2006, Journal of Cross-Cultural Psychology](https://doi.org/10.1177/0022022106292079). Children use eye direction to infer thinking and wanting, and autistic children do not — [Baron-Cohen et al. 1995, British Journal of Developmental Psychology](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2044-835x.1995.tb00687.x).
- Broad category evidence: 21 compound categories distinguishable by AU production — [Du et al. 2014, PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1322355111). 22 expressions across five cultures with a gradient of universality — [Cordaro et al. 2018, Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0000302). 18 facial-bodily expressions recognised above chance across nine cultures using story matching — [Cordaro et al. 2020, Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0000576). 28 dimensions of facial expression meaning from a network over 48 emotions and mental states, 21 with strong universality — [Brooks et al. 2024, iScience](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isci.2024.109175). A 2025 study of Chinese participants reported 14 emotions conveyed dynamically versus 10 statically — [Beyond the basic six, static, and WERID, JESP 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022103125001179) [snippet only; authors not verified].
- Appraisal route: observers detect appraisals from AUs, then label. Synthesised AU sets conveyed targeted appraisals — [Scherer et al. 2018, JPSP](https://doi.org/10.1037/pspa0000107). Actors produce predicted patterns and observers infer appraisals from viewers of adverts — [Scherer et al. 2021, Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0000693); [Sergi et al. 2016, IJSE](https://doi.org/10.4018/ijse.2016070103); multi-component percepts — [Shuman et al. 2017, Cognition and Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1080/02699931.2015.1075964); theory — [Scherer et al. 2013, Emotion Review](https://doi.org/10.1177/1754073912451504); [Scherer and Ellgring 2007, Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1037/1528-3542.7.1.158).
- Smile subtypes: amused, polite and embarrassed differ by morphology, timing and head pitch — [Ambadar et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10919-008-0059-5).
- Prediction for model: because EU-Emotion's face-only recoverability is label-specific, compare per-label human face accuracy with model accuracy. Humans fail on Kind and Unfriendly (around 9%) and Jealous, so a model scoring much higher on these should be suspected of leakage from actor or framing or non-facial features. Test by actor-holdout and by swapping backgrounds. Next, apply appraisal-defined AU edits: AU4 addition (goal obstruction) on neutral clips. Humans should move towards Frustrated, Worried, Disappointed-type labels. Then check whether the model's frustration responds to AU4 specifically (Grafsgaard, Scherer).
- Grade: moderate for Interested and frustration AUs; contested or thin for Worried, Bored, Disappointed. Evidence type is mainly CORR.

#### M22. The eye region: sufficient for complex states?
- For: with forced choice between two terms, eyes alone were as good as the whole face and better than the mouth for complex states; the whole face beat either part for basic emotions; autistic adults were most impaired with eyes alone — [Baron-Cohen et al. 1997, Visual Cognition](https://doi.org/10.1080/713756761) (posing actress, then an actor; 10 states). Revised test: [Baron-Cohen et al. 2001, Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry](https://doi.org/10.1111/1469-7610.00715). Eye aperture dimension: M5.
- Mixed or against: autistic groups did not differ from controls on eyes in isolation or in the whole face, and did better when eyes moved, undermining the "eyes deficit" claim — [Back et al. 2007, Child Development](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8624.2007.01005.x). For conversational expressions, single regions differ by expression and include mouth and head — [Cunningham et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1145/1077399.1077404); [Nusseck et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1167/8.8.1). For basic expressions the mouth carries most information — [Blais et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2012.08.010).
- Evidence type: STIM (old, small).
- Prediction for model: run EU-Emotion with eyes-only, mouth-only, and whole-face crops at matched input size, per label. Under Baron-Cohen 1997, complex labels hold under eyes-only and basic labels drop. Under Blais and Cunningham, complex labels are mixed. Human data on this exact split do not exist, so collect them: the eyes-only versus whole-face contrast is a primary preregistrable test, and a clean split of humans would also be publishable on its own terms.
- Grade: contested (COMPLEX).

#### M23. RMET validity and verbal load
- Verbal IQ explained RMET variance in 42 students, while a face perception test did not — [Peterson and Miller 2012, Frontiers in Psychology](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2012.00220). Strong link with vocabulary; recommended short form and reduced reliance on vocabulary — [Olderbak et al. 2015, Frontiers in Psychology](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.01503). Intelligence meta-analysis — [Baker et al. 2014, Intelligence](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.intell.2014.03.001) [meta]. Only estimated IQ, no executive domain, predicted RMET — [Ahmed and Miller 2011, JADD](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10803-010-1087-7).
- Meta-analysis: RMET related more to emotion perception (r = .33, rho = .48) than to other ToM measures (r = .29, rho = .39), and to vocabulary (r = .25, rho = .32); internal consistency alpha .73; authors conclude it acts as an emotion perception measure — [Kittel et al. 2022, Assessment](https://doi.org/10.1177/1073191121996469).
- Alexithymia, not autism diagnosis, drove RMET effect, while autistic participants were impaired on MASC — [Oakley et al. 2016, Journal of Abnormal Psychology](https://doi.org/10.1037/abn0000182). Education, race and ethnicity effects were more pronounced than in other tasks, suggesting cultural loading — [Dodell-Feder et al. preprint](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/v73jm).
- Poor factor fit in large representative US sample; nearly a quarter of items fail the original inclusion criterion — [Higgins et al. 2023, Assessment](https://doi.org/10.1177/10731911221124342). In a scoping review, 63% of RMET studies reported no validity evidence — [Higgins et al. 2024, Clinical Psychology Review](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2023.102378). Threats to validity — [Higgins et al. 2023, Neuroscience and Biobehavioral Reviews](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2023.105088) [meta], commenting on [Pavlova and Sokolov 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2022.104755) [meta]. ToM measures lack specificity — [Quesque and Rossetti 2020, Perspectives on Psychological Science](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691619896607).
- Executive load: Eyes showed specific cost with an inhibitory secondary task, not across all executive tasks — [Bull et al. 2008, Cognition](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2007.07.015). Note conflict with Ahmed and Miller's null on executive predictors (correlational versus dual-task).
- Norms and alternatives: 57-country female advantage — [Greenberg et al. 2022, PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2022385119). Multiracial version, open data at osf.io/ahq6n, reliable signal indistinguishable from RMET — [Kim et al. 2024, Behavior Research Methods](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-023-02323-x). Genetic correlation data — [Warrier et al. 2017, Molecular Psychiatry](https://doi.org/10.1038/mp.2017.122) (abstract retrieved, not relied on here).
- Evidence type: CORR.
- Prediction for model: an LLM-backed model has near-perfect vocabulary, which inverts the human constraint. Predicted signature: human RMET errors correlate with word difficulty, model errors do not, and pure language priors can do well. Three tests. (a) Text-only control: give the model the four words with no image; above-chance scoring means language prior leakage. (b) Foil swap: same image, replacement foils matched for frequency or synonyms; a percept-based answer survives and a word-prior answer does not. (c) Item-difficulty correlation with human norms, partialling word frequency. Use the MRMET as an open replication set.
- Grade: strong that RMET is contaminated by verbal ability and emotion perception, with effect sizes small to moderate; contested on what is left.

#### M23b. Conceptual knowledge shapes perception (Dynamic Interactive Theory, constructionism)
- Individuals' conceptual similarity between emotions predicts mouse-trajectory attraction between categories and resemblance of reverse-correlated prototypes, controlling physical similarity — [Brooks and Freeman 2018, Nature Human Behaviour](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-018-0376-6). Right fusiform pattern similarity follows the subject's conceptual structure, and explains cultural differences — [Brooks et al. 2019, PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1816408116). Sex stereotypes bias visual prototypes (male-angry, female-happy) — [Brooks et al. 2018, Social Cognition](https://doi.org/10.1521/soco.2018.36.5.481).
- Theory: Dynamic Interactive model of person construal — [Freeman and Ambady 2011, Psychological Review](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0022327); [Freeman et al. 2020, Advances in Experimental Social Psychology](https://doi.org/10.1016/bs.aesp.2019.09.005); [Freeman and Johnson 2016, TiCS](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2016.03.003) [abstract not read]; trait spaces shift by context — [Stolier et al. 2018, TiCS](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2017.12.003).
- Semantic dementia: three patients perceived valence but not discrete emotions, even without using words — [Lindquist et al. 2014, Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0035293). Small n, degenerative disease with broad deficits.
- Language: [Lindquist et al. 2015, Current Directions](https://doi.org/10.1177/0963721414553440). Emotion words bias perceptual memory, even with novel alien expressions — [Doyle and Lindquist 2018, JEP General](https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0000361); stronger when structural information is limited — [Fugate et al. 2018, Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0000330). Verbal knowledge mediates development from valence to valence-and-arousal representation over ages 6-25 — [Nook et al. 2017, Nature Human Behaviour](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-017-0238-7). Children's categories grow from valence — [Widen 2013, Emotion Review](https://doi.org/10.1177/1754073912451492). Hoemann's hypothesis: emotion categories are abstract and learned via words labelling variable instances — [Hoemann et al. 2019, Developmental Psychology](https://doi.org/10.1037/dev0000686); [Hoemann et al. 2020, Emotion Review](https://doi.org/10.1177/1754073919897296). Cross-language structure varies but is constrained — [Jackson et al. 2019, Science](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aaw8160). Constructionist statements: [Barrett 2006, Perspectives on Psychological Science](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1745-6916.2006.00003.x); [Barrett 2017, SCAN](https://doi.org/10.1093/scan/nsx060).
- Counter-position: categorisation depends more on perceptual than affective mechanisms and emotionless computational models reproduce phenomena — [Calvo and Nummenmaa 2016, Cognition and Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1080/02699931.2015.1049124). Label-free CP in Yucatec Maya — [Sauter et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0025336).
- Cognitive load: verbal working memory load interferes with choosing a label, grows with number of labels, and spares same-different discrimination — [Phillips et al. 2008, Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1037/1528-3542.8.2.184). Most expressions, including self-conscious ones, are recognised under load and deliberation adds little — [Tracy and Robins 2008, Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1037/1528-3542.8.1.81).
- Evidence type: CORR, OBS (few patients), STIM (word learning).
- Prediction for model: compute each model's own text-embedding similarity among the 21 labels (concept structure) and regress its visual confusions on that structure, partialling image similarity. Brooks and Freeman predict that human confusion RDM is predicted by human conceptual RDM. Test: does the model's confusion matrix match its text similarity more than human confusion does? Intervention: a prompt redefining "Worried" as a synonym of "Bored" or giving altered definitions should move the model's perceptual boundary; for humans, concept training moves perceptual memory modestly. If a definition change moves model output a lot while image degradation moves it little, the model is concept-led. The repository's `data/human_confusion_rdm.npy` and `data/eu_emotion_definitions.json` support this directly.
- Grade: moderate for conceptual structure predicting perception (mostly BASIC, one lab, correlational); contested for constructionist claim that concepts are necessary; not directly tested for complex labels.

### Inferences
- Key structural hypothesis: EU-Emotion labels fall into at least three classes. High-face labels (joking, surprised, happy) are signalled. Low-face, high-body labels (kind, unfriendly, disappointed, frustrated) are poorly signalled by face alone, at least for some of them. Situational labels (jealous, 44% from social scenes, 14% from faces) require context. Evidence for Disappointed face accuracy is not in the retrieved text, so this needs Table 2.
- Three labels in the study (Kind, Unfriendly, Jealous) had near-floor human face recognition. Scoring models on them against human accuracy without a human noise ceiling is misleading: a chance-level model may be human-like.
- For complex labels, "which pixels" may be less apt than "which appraisal". Inverse-appraisal prompts (M26) and AU edits should be tested together.

### Gaps
- No located reverse-correlation model for Bored, Worried, Disappointed, Kind or Unfriendly specifically. Liu et al. 2022 covers 19 complex emotions but the abstract does not list them; check the list against EU-Emotion labels.
- Craig 2008 full AU rules not retrieved; the abstract confirms only that rules exist. A search result said confusion associates with AU4 or AU7, but its source was secondary.
- No causal manipulation of cues for Disappointed located; no eyes-only versus whole-face human study on EU-Emotion; no 2023-2026 human RMET whole-face comparison located.
- Sneaky, Hurt, Joking and Excited: no specific cue literature located in this pass.
- Lindquist 2014 and Brooks 2018 concern basic emotions; extension to complex states is theoretical.

## Context effects: how far does the face alone go?

### Takeaway
Context can override faces, categorically for ambiguous or intense expressions, automatically and even under load. But the size of the override depends on face ambiguity, task and relevance, and silent face clips remove most of it. This matters for EU-Emotion because the face-clip stimuli were separated from the body and scene modalities where several labels are far better recognised.

### Cited Findings

#### M24. Body and scene context
- The same disgust face is read differently on bodies conveying other emotions, and context alters fixation patterns from the first fixation — [Aviezer et al. 2008, Psychological Science](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02148.x). Intense positive and negative faces cannot be separated by face but can by body, and viewers perceive illusory valence in the face — [Aviezer et al. 2012, Science](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1224313). Integration is unintentional and survives load — [Aviezer et al. 2011, Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0023578). Facial reactions in intense situations are often not diagnostic of valence — [Wenzler et al. 2016, Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0000185). Olympic athletes: context overrides even for valence — [Kayyal et al. 2015, Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0000032). Real-life highly positive reactions: faces are poorly coupled to feeling — [Israelashvili et al. 2019, Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0000441). Situation beat face in all 22 cases, especially for non-basic emotions — [Carroll and Russell 1996, JPSP](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.70.2.205). Review: [Hassin et al. 2013, Emotion Review](https://doi.org/10.1177/1754073912451331).
- Limits: context influence varies with cue type, relevance instruction and age, and instructions to treat context as irrelevant reduce it — [Ngo and Isaacowitz 2015, Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0000062). Unique and interactive contributions of faces, postures and scenes — [Reschke and Walle 2021, Affective Science](https://doi.org/10.1007/s42761-021-00061-x). Different tasks give very different cue-weightings in Bayesian integration, due to decision processes — [Anzellotti et al. 2021, Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0000685).
- Context alone: observers track affect of an invisible, masked character from visual context, with unique contribution beyond face and body — [Chen and Whitney 2019, PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1812250116), as quickly as with all information — [Chen and Whitney 2021, Cognition](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2020.104549). Commentary: [Martinez 2019, PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1902661116). Computer vision precedent: EMOTIC, 26 categories plus VAD, person-plus-scene model — [Kosti et al. 2017, CVPR](https://doi.org/10.1109/cvpr.2017.212).
- Kuleshov: neutral faces read per framing; amygdala link — [Mobbs et al. 2006, SCAN](https://doi.org/10.1093/scan/nsl014); supported in a replication — [Barratt et al. 2016, Perception](https://doi.org/10.1177/0301006616638595); significant for fear — [Calbi et al. 2017, Frontiers in Psychology](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.01684). 
- Encoding: context is routinely encoded when labelling emotion, more than when judging affect — [Barrett and Kensinger 2010, Psychological Science](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797610363547). Scene congruency speeds recognition under load — [Righart and de Gelder 2008, CABN](https://doi.org/10.3758/cabn.8.3.264). A peripheral face's expression and gaze change target recognition, beyond mere context (social appraisal) — [Mumenthaler and Sander 2012, JPSP](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0026885); culture — [Masuda et al. 2008, JPSP](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.94.3.365). Review: [Wieser and Brosch 2012, Frontiers in Psychology](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2012.00471).
- Evidence type: STIM.
- Prediction for model: because face clips are silent and bodyless, inject context by text: "She has just lost the final." or "Her friend has just surprised her." before an ambiguous clip. Humans: large shift for ambiguous clips, little for clear ones, and a shift still present when told the sentence may be irrelevant, reduced but not removed (Ngo). Also paste the same face on a body or scene with conflicting emotion and read the label. A model fails by ignoring context entirely, or by following text regardless of face (text dominance). The second failure is more likely for language-heavy models, so the relevant measure is the slope of context weight against face clarity.
- Grade: strong (BOTH, though mostly basic or valence); complex-state text context effects are supported by Carroll and Russell.

#### M25. Reverse-inference limits (Barrett 2019 and successors)
- People do sometimes smile when happy more than chance, but configurations vary by culture, situation and person and are not specific to one category — [Barrett et al. 2019, Psychological Science in the Public Interest](https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100619832930). Actors posing rich scenarios use variable configurations; only fear, happiness and surprise had moderate reliability and specificity, and perceived emotion is context-sensitive — [Le Mau et al. 2021, Nature Communications](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-25352-6). Meta-analytic co-occurrence numbers are in M1. Variable facial patterns in affect bursts — [Krumhuber and Scherer 2011, Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0023856).
- Humans recognise subtle nonprototypical expressions better than a classifier — [Yitzhak et al. 2017, Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0000287). Automated recognition falls with less prototypical, more naturalistic databases — [Krumhuber et al. 2021, Behavior Research Methods](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-020-01443-y). 
- Social signal alternative: faces as social tools, not emotion readouts — [Crivelli and Fridlund 2018, TiCS](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2018.02.006); solitary smiling tracks implicit audience — [Fridlund 1991, JPSP](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.60.2.229). Cross-cultural debate: [Russell 1994, Psychological Bulletin](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.115.1.102); [Ekman 1994, Psychological Bulletin](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.115.2.268); [Elfenbein and Ambady 2002, Psychological Bulletin](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.128.2.203); [Gendron et al. 2014, Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0036052); [Gendron et al. 2018, Current Directions](https://doi.org/10.1177/0963721417746794); [Crivelli et al. 2016, PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1611622113). Counterpoint on contexts: 16 expressions linked to contexts with about 70% preservation across regions — [Cowen et al. 2021, Nature](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-020-3037-7).
- Evidence type: CORR.
- Prediction for model: EU-Emotion is acted portrayal under a label. Accuracy reflects shared convention, not felt state. Humans and models trained on posed, stereotyped data should do best for prototypical clips. Fit accuracy versus AU prototypicality per clip. Yitzhak predicts humans keep accuracy on nonprototypical clips and models drop. Crossing this line (model falls off faster) is a predicted human-model difference.
- Grade: strong that felt-state inference is weak for BASIC; for perception of acted COMPLEX signals, the relevance is an inference.

### Inferences
- The study should frame results as recognising acted conventions, not reading felt states. That wording avoids Barrett-type critique.
- Context weight as a function of face ambiguity is a compact, comparable parameter across humans and models.

### Gaps
- No located study of context effects on EU-Emotion stimuli, and none using EU-Emotion's complex labels with text context.
- No located study testing text-context with silent face video on humans for labels such as Worried and Disappointed. A human arm is needed.

## Theory: inverse appraisal, Gershman, computational psychiatry, and what selects a route

### Takeaway
The best formal account treats emotion attribution as Bayesian inversion of an intuitive causal model linking situations, goals, appraisals, emotions and expressions. It is well supported on situation-to-emotion inference, less so on face video. No located Gershman paper addresses facial emotion recognition; his relevant contribution is the arbitration and theory-of-mind machinery. Autism accounts offer a usable contrast (eye avoidance, reduced prior use), but Bayesian evidence is weaker than the theory's prominence suggests. Route choice in humans appears to follow cue reliability, task and label set, and this is a synthesis, not a tested law.

### Cited Findings

#### M26. Inverse appraisal and inverse planning
- Affective cognition: a lay theory relates events and emotional responses, and emotional cue integration is rational Bayes; the model closely tracks judgements — [Ong et al. 2015, Cognition](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2015.06.010). Roadmap: [Ong et al. 2019, Topics in Cognitive Science](https://doi.org/10.1111/tops.12371). Stimulus features alone support limited attribution, whereas specific granular emotions need inferences about appraisal against goals, beliefs, moral values — [Saxe and Houlihan 2017, Current Opinion in Psychology](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.copsyc.2017.04.019).
- Formal model: inverse planning infers beliefs and preferences (including social ones) from one choice in a high-stakes Split or Steal game, computes appraisals and maps them to 20 emotions, matching human predictions; social preferences matter for nearly all emotions — [Houlihan et al. 2023, Phil Trans R Soc A](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsta.2022.0047) [PMC10239682, snippet of abstract]. Foundation: [Baker et al. 2009, Cognition](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2009.07.005); naive utility calculus [Jara-Ettinger et al. 2016, TiCS](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2016.08.007) [meta]. Reverse direction, from emotion to mental state: Bayesian model captured judgements (r at least .95) — [Wu et al. 2018, Cognitive Science](https://doi.org/10.1111/cogs.12548). Emotions shown by agents let people infer others' appraisal and intentions in strategic games — [de Melo et al. 2014, JPSP](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0034251). Reverse-engineered appraisals drive person inferences: anger responses to blame imply aggression and less warmth than sadness — [Hareli and Hess 2010, Cognition and Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1080/02699930802613828). This bears on Unfriendly and Kind.
- Neural support: mentalising regions code fine emotions from situation descriptions, along abstract event features — [Skerry and Saxe 2015, Current Biology](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2015.06.009). Common code for perceived and inferred emotion — [Skerry and Saxe 2014, Journal of Neuroscience](https://doi.org/10.1523/jneurosci.1676-14.2014). Mental-state dimensions rationality, social impact, valence — [Tamir et al. 2016, PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1511905112). Transitions: [Thornton and Tamir 2017, PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1616056114).
- Evidence type: computational fit plus CORR; few causal face studies.
- Prediction for model: ask for appraisal ratings of each clip (goal blocked? expected? who is responsible?) and test whether appraisal outputs predict labels better than AU outputs do (mediation). Humans under Scherer and Saxe route: appraisal mediates complex labels, less so for basic. Intervene: edit the described situation so the same outcome is expected versus unexpected; Disappointed needs an unmet expectation, so appraisal theory predicts shifting to Sad-type readings when told the event was expected. A model fails if it does not revise by expectation. Check the model's chain-of-reasoning text against this, knowing text may be post hoc.
- Grade: strong for situation-to-emotion inference; moderate for face-to-appraisal (Scherer); contested as the route in fast recognition from video.

#### M27. Gershman-relevant work, and route selection as arbitration
- I reviewed Gershman's publication list directly: no title addresses facial expression or emotion recognition. Nearest items concern anxiety and anhedonia (for example Fan et al. 2023, Bari and Gershman 2025) — [Gershman lab publications](https://gershmanlab.com/pubs.html).
- Relevant: people's theory of mind includes a theory of others' habitual action as well as planned action, and is sensitive to factors affecting their balance — [Gershman et al. 2016, PLOS ONE](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0162246). Note this concerns how observers model others, not a habit route in the observer. Cost-benefit arbitration between systems — [Kool et al. 2017, Psychological Science](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797617708288). Theory of mind arbitrating social and non-social learning — [Ying et al. 2025 preprint](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/4ctjp_v1). Computational rationality — [Gershman et al. 2015, Science](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aac6076). Resource-rational framing — [Lieder and Griffiths 2020, BBS](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0140525x1900061x); strategy selection — [Lieder and Griffiths 2017, Psychological Review](https://doi.org/10.1037/rev0000075). Social structure learning: [Gershman et al. 2017, Cognitive Science](https://doi.org/10.1111/cogs.12480); [Gershman and Cikara 2020, Current Directions](https://doi.org/10.1177/0963721420924481). Mood as momentum (Eldar, not Gershman) — [Eldar et al. 2016, TiCS](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2015.07.010).
- Direct evidence on route choice in social perception:
  - Cue reliability: gaze matters only if the expression is hard or mild (M6). Motion matters when degraded or subtle (M8). Context matters when faces are ambiguous (M24). Emotion words matter when structure is limited — [Fugate et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0000330).
  - Bayesian cue integration framework — [Zaki 2013, Perspectives on Psychological Science](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691613475454).
  - Conflict engages domain-general control, and biases mirror or mentalising systems according to which cue gets weight — [Zaki et al. 2010, Journal of Neuroscience](https://doi.org/10.1523/jneurosci.0382-10.2010). Both systems track accuracy — [Zaki et al. 2009, PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0902666106).
  - Load: mirror-system behaviour identification persists, mentalising with a why-goal is removed by load — [Spunt and Lieberman 2013, Psychological Science](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797612450884). Label naming is load-sensitive, matching is not — [Phillips et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1037/1528-3542.8.2.184). Review of emotion-understanding layers — [Spunt and Adolphs 2019, Neuroscience Letters](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neulet.2017.06.018). Hierarchical cognitive and affective routes — [Schurz et al. 2021, Psychological Bulletin](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000303).
  - Task: information use follows the category set — [Smith and Merlusca 2014](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0035588). Scanpaths are task and person specific — [Kanan et al. 2015, Vision Research](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.visres.2015.01.013).
- Evidence type: mostly CORR plus some STIM; the arbitration claim is an inference for face tasks.
- Prediction for model: a reliability-weighted route predicts an interaction. Vary face signal (noise, intensity) crossed with context availability, and fit context weight as a function of face reliability. Humans should give a rising curve. Do the same for gaze and dynamics. A model with a constant weight has fixed integration, not arbitration. Time pressure analogue: reduced tokens or reduced temperature has no clear human mapping, so avoid this.
- Grade: moderate (inferred); contested as a unified account.

#### M28. Autism: strategies, eye use, priors
- Face scanning and information use: autistic adults failed to use eye information and relied on the mouth (Bubbles) — [Spezio et al. 2007, JADD](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10803-006-0232-9). Mouth bias is top-down, not saliency, and appears even if the mouth is absent — [Neumann et al. 2006, SCAN](https://doi.org/10.1093/scan/nsl030). Early scanning differences — [Pelphrey et al. 2002, JADD](https://doi.org/10.1023/a:1016374617369). Mouth fixation predicted better social adjustment — [Klin et al. 2002, Archives of General Psychiatry](https://doi.org/10.1001/archpsyc.59.9.809). Dynamic faces: all groups fixate eyes most; HFA modestly less — [Macinska et al. 2023, JADD](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10803-023-05979-8). Fixations were typical and did not explain reduced emotion specificity — [Wang and Adolphs 2017, Neuropsychologia](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2017.03.024). Reduced sensitivity and reliability of ratings — [Kennedy and Adolphs 2012, Neuropsychologia](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2012.09.038). Heterogeneity across individuals, reliability within — [Keles et al. 2022, Molecular Autism](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13229-022-00517-2). Pixel-level over semantic saliency — [Wang et al. 2015, Neuron](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2015.09.042).
- Eye avoidance versus reduced orienting: gaze correlates with amygdala activation in autism — [Dalton et al. 2005, Nature Neuroscience](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn1421). Eye avoidance hypothesis — [Tanaka and Sung 2016, JADD](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10803-013-1976-7). Constraining gaze to the eyes produces abnormally high subcortical activation — [Hadjikhani et al. 2017, Scientific Reports](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-017-03378-5); also with low intensity fear — [Lassalle et al. 2017, Human Brain Mapping](https://doi.org/10.1002/hbm.23800). Eight of eleven reviewed studies supported eye avoidance — [Stuart et al. 2023, JADD](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10803-022-05443-z). Amygdala involvement in both — [Kliemann et al. 2012, Journal of Neuroscience](https://doi.org/10.1523/jneurosci.5294-11.2012). Open-label bumetanide — [Hadjikhani et al. 2018, Scientific Reports](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-018-21958-x), uncontrolled.
- Bayesian accounts: hypo-priors — [Pellicano and Burr 2012, TiCS](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2012.08.009); precision of prediction errors — [Van de Cruys et al. 2014, Psychological Review](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0037665); aberrant precision — [Lawson et al. 2014, Frontiers in Human Neuroscience](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2014.00302); overlearning volatility — [Lawson et al. 2017, Nature Neuroscience](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4615); review — [Palmer et al. 2017, Psychological Bulletin](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000097). Ten-year systematic review: results highly mixed, a slight majority of studies found no difference in integrating priors, reduced influence was more frequent for priors learned in the experiment, and low power was common — [Angeletos Chrysaitis and Seriès 2023, Neuroscience and Biobehavioral Reviews](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2022.105022). Different causal inference with explicit compensation — [Noel et al. 2022, eLife](https://doi.org/10.7554/elife.71866).
- Face-specific priors and adaptation: reduced face adaptation — [Pellicano et al. 2007, Current Biology](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2007.07.065). Expression aftereffects reduced with intact norm-based coding — [Rhodes et al. 2018, British Journal of Psychology](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjop.12257). Autistic adults had more precise emotion representations that did not improve recognition, consistent with less guidance by representation — [Keating et al. 2022 preprint](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/3hbr2). Rule-based, not template-based, processing of exaggerated faces — [Rutherford and McIntosh 2007, JADD](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10803-006-0151-9).
- Size and specificity: impairment across all basic emotions, with moderators of emotion complexity and holistic processing, and not specific to emotion or to faces — [Yeung 2022, Neuroscience and Biobehavioral Reviews](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2021.104518); [Lozier et al. 2014, Development and Psychopathology](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0954579414000479); [Uljarevic and Hamilton 2013, JADD](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10803-012-1695-5); [Harms et al. 2010, Neuropsychology Review](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11065-010-9138-6). Majority with significant deficits on a film test; 15.3% within 1 SD — [Loth et al. 2018, Molecular Autism](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13229-018-0187-7). Basic and complex deficits in EU-Emotion-based tasks across three cultures, complex harder for all children — [Fridenson-Hayo et al. 2016, Molecular Autism](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13229-016-0113-9).
- Alexithymia alternative: alexithymia, not autism, predicted recognition difficulty — [Cook et al. 2013, Psychological Science](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797612463582); review [Bird and Cook 2013, Translational Psychiatry](https://doi.org/10.1038/tp.2013.61); but anger from motion stays different after alexithymia control — [Keating et al. 2020 preprint](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-88768/v2). Autistic expression production is idiosyncratic — [Brewer et al. 2016, Autism Research](https://doi.org/10.1002/aur.1508). Motion kinematics: [Sowden et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0000835).
- Evidence type: CORR, some STIM; forced gaze is OBS-like.
- Prediction for model: a "feature-based, mouth-weighted, weak-template" profile is a fittable pattern rather than a diagnosis. Compare model confusions with neurotypical and autistic human confusions where available, and fit weight on eyes versus mouth and on prior (label frequency) sensitivity. Change label base rates in few-shot examples: humans use priors, and an autistic-profile prediction is lower sensitivity. A model insensitive to base rate is prior-blind. Avoid implying that a model "is autistic-like": the analogy is at the information-use level only.
- Grade: moderate for reduced eye use and adaptation; contested for hypo-priors and for eye avoidance versus reduced orienting; autism versus alexithymia is contested.

### Inferences
- A workable taxonomy of three human routes for the paper: (R1) signal reading: AU and motion to label, fast, load-tolerant, strongest for high-face labels; (R2) appraisal and context inference: situation, gaze, goals, for labels needing a cause; (R3) conceptual and verbal: label semantics, emphasised by forced choice and verbal load, as in RMET. The prediction is that VLMs overweight R3 and underweight R1 dynamics. This taxonomy is my synthesis from M2, M21, M23, M23b and M26, and is not an established model.
- Supervisor's "what makes a human do a specific task": available evidence says the option set and instruction change the information used (Smith and Merlusca; Phillips; Schyns and Oliva). So 6AFC with None of the above is itself a manipulation, and should be crossed with free response.
- Gershman's arbitration work (Kool et al.) offers a testable analogy for observers: a cheap cached label given a smile versus costly inference from a situation. Applying it to faces is my extrapolation, and the 2016 plans-and-habits paper is about the attributed agent rather than the perceiver.

### Gaps
- No located paper applying inverse planning or formal arbitration to face video. No located Gershman paper on facial emotion.
- No verified report of Lassalle eye-tracking data on EU-Emotion stimuli. The located Lassalle work (2017, 2019 voice database, 2015 with Itier) does not establish it; see the next section.
- Autism priors have not been tested with complex-state video in any located study.

## Measures of individual differences, and whether AU or eye-tracking annotations of EU-Emotion or similar stimuli exist

### Takeaway
Reliable tests exist for individual differences, with a partly general and partly face-specific ability structure. I found no published AU coding or eye-tracking dataset for EU-Emotion; the validation paper reports none, and the local repository holds none outside these notes. Annotation will need to be generated, ideally alongside a human arm. Close alternatives with published norms exist.

Other inferences:
- Because all human predictions come from other stimuli, the causal claim requires a matched human experiment. A sensible minimal version is a Prolific-style study of ranks 1 to 5 on a balanced subset. I did not design or cost this here.
- Label reliability should weight every comparison. Kind, Unfriendly and Jealous near-floor from faces should not be treated as equal to Happy.
- Discrepancy to reconcile: the brief says 243 clips, 21 labels, 6AFC. Repository documents (`docs/ARTIFACT.md`, `docs/HUMAN_RDM.md`) describe 118 trials, 27 labels and 4AFC. `data/eu_emotion_states_list.txt` lists 27 entries because it splits out low-intensity variants. The paper reports 249 face clips and 6AFC. Confirm which applies before quoting human norms. Those documents may simply predate the current design.
- Partial actor-label crossing (17 actors, each 10 emotions plus neutral) makes it important to fit actor effects, and limits leave-one-actor-out within labels.
- AU extraction with OpenFace or similar is feasible for EU-Emotion, but such tools are trained on basic prototypes and will be weakest on subtle complex clips. That risk follows from the Krumhuber 2021 and Yitzhak 2017 pattern; I did not validate it on EU-Emotion.

- Absence of evidence: I could not search Google Scholar directly or access paywalled full texts of many papers, so an EU-Emotion AU or eye-tracking paper could exist unseen.
